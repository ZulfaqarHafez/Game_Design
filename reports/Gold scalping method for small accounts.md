# Costs kill gold scalps; pullbacks barely survive

No 5-minute scalping method for gold shows a real edge after realistic retail costs: not in academic studies, not in independent practitioner tests, and not in our own data, where every 5-minute rule we measured came out negative or indistinguishable from zero. The best-evidenced rule-based method, and the one to test-trade, is a **15-minute trend pullback**: trade only with the 1-hour 200-EMA trend, enter when price pulls back to the 21-EMA and closes back across the 9-EMA, risk 2.5 ATR and target twice that. In our own backtest it was the only pre-specified candidate that stayed positive after costs, at **+0.21R per trade ±0.20 (R is the amount risked to the stop), profit factor 1.15, 215 trades from January 2025 to May 2026**, and its controls behaved as a real effect should. It is the best available candidate, not a proven one: the project's own readiness checklist rates it **"Not ready"** because it lost money in the most recent 30% of the data, the confidence interval barely clears zero after about 45 backtest runs, and its only published support is a self-reported 1-hour system. It is also not a scalp: it wins about 40% of the time, holds for about seven hours and trades about three times a week. On a $100 account in Singapore, regulated brokers require 20% margin on gold, so the only position that fits is 0.1 oz; run that way, the backtest **made $39 in 16 months with a $53 drawdown**, about $0.18 a trade against roughly $1.50 to $2 at risk. The $100 buys a clean experiment, not a growth account: run the saved rule on the unseen May to November 2026 data first, then paper-trade it, then trade it live at minimum size with a stop rule fixed in advance and no verdict before 100 trades.

## A $0.80 round trip swallows a quarter of every 5-minute bar

One number sets the terms for every method. On the project's Dukascopy bid/ask history, a market-order round trip in gold costs about **$0.75 to $0.80 per ounce** all-in: the recorded spread plus $0.07 commission and $0.10 slippage. That is **17–31% of the median 5-minute bar** in liquid London and New York hours, 57–62% in the hours either side of the daily break at 17:00 New York, and 10–17% of a 15-minute bar ([our own data](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/own_data_statistics.md)). The dollar spread has tracked the gold price: the median on that feed rose from $0.38 in 2024 to $0.71 in January–September 2026, and the 95th percentile tripled from $0.72 to $2.14 ([our own computation](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/costs_small_account_mechanics.md)). A Singapore spread-only account could be cheaper, an estimated $0.40 to $0.50 all-in at IG's advertised 0.3 minimum, but that figure is a floor and no regulated broker's live 2026 gold spread could be verified ([IG Singapore](https://www.ig.com/sg/help-and-support/articles/720379-what-are-ig-s-commodities-cfd-product-details)). Volatility has also roughly halved since the January–April 2026 blow-off while dollar spreads fell only slightly, so cost as a share of a bar is now 1.5 to 2 times what it was in early 2026; late 2025 is the closest measured analogue to today.

The arithmetic of small wins is unforgiving. With target T, stop S and cost c, the break-even win rate is (S + c) / (T + S). A $3 target with a $3 stop needs 57.5% winners at a $0.45 cost and 60% at $0.60. A $2 target with a $4 stop, the classic many-small-wins shape, needs 74%. A $10 target with a $5 stop needs 36–37% ([our own arithmetic](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/costs_small_account_mechanics.md)). High win rates come from that geometry, not from skill. In an independent test of 91,943 trades per variant across 12 markets, a range fade with a target half the size of its stop **won 63.1% of the time and still lost 0.051R per trade**, which is what a random walk delivers when the target sits half as far away as the stop ([Schuch, MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/779464)). Grid and martingale "scalpers" push the same trade-off to its limit: one gold grid robot on default settings took a $1,000 account to $57 on a roughly 1% move after 1,589 winning baskets in a row ([Arribas Lopez, MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778258)), and in a 2015–2026 tester run of 43 popular paid gold robots, 19% of those that could be measured blew up ([Sakamoto, MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/773167)). That family is excluded from everything below.

There is also no raw signal on 5-minute bars for costs to eat. In our own data, variance ratios sit between 0.90 and 1.03 in every session and lag-1 autocorrelation between −0.02 and +0.02; the London morning matched the direction of the New York morning on 50% of days ([our own data](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/own_data_statistics.md)). Independent work agrees. Across 432 scalping-rule versions on 1- and 5-minute data for nine markets including XAUUSD, one was significant in-sample and none out-of-sample; breakouts, RSI(2) reversion and VWAP fades were about zero before costs and lost 0.2 to 2.0 basis points per trade after them ([Schuch, MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/779464)). A separate cost-aware test of gold on 5-minute bars from 2016 to 2026 passed none of 400-plus variants and concluded: "Most intraday ideas are zero before costs. Costs then make them negative" ([Nambala, MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)). The one peer-reviewed rule study on 5-minute gold covered 66,297 moving-average crossovers over 2008–2014: the popular fast settings lost money before costs, and only slow simple-average rules held in and out of sample, the best pairing a 44-bar average with a 332-bar one, about 4 hours against 28 ([CXO Advisory summary of Urquhart et al.](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=27613)).

The base rates point the same way. Of Brazilians who day-traded index futures for more than 300 days, 97% lost money ([Chague et al., SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). Fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders reliably earn positive returns after fees ([Barber et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)). European regulators found 74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)), and India's regulator found the losing share rises from 71% to 80% among the most frequent intraday traders ([ThePrint on SEBI](https://theprint.in/economy/71-of-intraday-traders-incur-losses-finds-sebi-report-impact-higher-among-young-small-traders/2192598)). When the United States capped retail FX leverage in 2010, high-leverage traders' losses fell by 40% ([Heimer and Simsek, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24176)). No study isolates $100 accounts or gold scalping, but frequent, leveraged, intraday and small is the worst cell in every dataset that exists.

## Seven method families tested, one survivor after costs

We weighed seven families against three kinds of evidence: academic studies, practitioner tests (weighted by whether they include costs, controls and an author with nothing to sell), and two sets of our own tests. **The own tests are not web-sourced.** They were computed on 19 November 2026 with this project's code and backtest engine from Dukascopy XAUUSD bid bars with the recorded spread: 5- and 15-minute bars from 1 January 2025 to 3 May 2026 (343 trading days) and 1-hour bars from 2019 to September 2026, at realistic costs of about $0.75–0.80 per ounce. The candidate rules were written down from the research notes before any run, each with a control that inverts or removes its directional filter. Two caveats apply to every own-test number below. The intraday sample is a single 16 months of an unusually volatile bull-then-correction market, and about 45 strategy runs were made, so a result has to clear a higher bar than a lone 95% interval ([own statistics](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/own_data_statistics.md); [candidate backtests](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/candidate_backtests.md)). Profit factor (PF) is gross profit divided by gross loss; "±" figures are 95% confidence intervals.

Three patterns explain the table. The breakout family has a gross edge of about 0.1R or less, and the cost of a tight stop is 6–12% of risk, the same size ([Krueger](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). Our New York opening-range test has exactly that shape: a gross profit factor of 1.32 falls to 1.03 net, and trading against the opening candle did no worse. The reversal family has no gross edge to protect, since the sweep, the fake-out and the band fade are coin flips on gold in every independent sample and in ours. And the two effects with peer-reviewed support are real but tiny. A 1% move by 13:00 New York predicts about one basis point of further drift into the old pit close ([Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)), and the Asian-hours drift is worth about $0.68 an ounce gross against a cost of about $0.80 on our data.

The vendor results that contradict this picture share the same defects: windows of a year or so inside the 2023–2026 bull run, re-fitted parameters, and missing spread or slippage. The most visible gold opening-range system, advertised as up 411% in a year, was re-fitted in February and April 2026 and shelved by its author in July when its break statistics fell to "nearly 50/50" ([TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/)). A cost-aware tester found a free gold breakout robot's +0.21R per trade was mostly matched by random entries with the same exits (+0.14R): "Most of its profit was the bull market" ([Nambala](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)). The research found no verified live account with a long history and no martingale for any gold breakout, pullback or reversal scalper.

What the surviving results share is structural, not clever: a higher-timeframe trend filter, a volatility-scaled stop wide enough that cost is a few percent of risk, and holds of hours. The academic record points to the same family. The only gold moving-average rules that held out of sample were slow ones, and large intraday moves in commodity futures tend to continue the same day, not reverse ([Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)).

## The 15-minute trend pullback made +0.21R, and our own checklist says not ready

In our own backtest at realistic costs, the pre-specified pullback rule made **+0.21R per trade (95% interval +0.01R to +0.41R), a net profit factor of 1.15 and +$1.83 per ounce per trade over 215 trades**, about three a week. Three features separate it from the other candidates. The trend filter carries the result in the right order: trend-aligned +0.21R, no 1-hour filter +0.03R, counter-trend −0.26R, the reverse of the breakout and sweep tests, where the controls matched or beat the rule. Costs barely touch it because the stop is wide. Median risk is $15.6 an ounce, so an $0.80 round trip is about 5% of risk, and doubling costs leaves +0.20R and a profit factor of 1.14. And nearby settings form a plateau, not a spike: stops of 2.0 to 3.0 ATR against targets of 1.5 to 2.5R gave +0.07R to +0.24R in all nine combinations, although profit factor ranged from 0.92 to 1.19 and two combinations sat at or below 1.03. Longs (+0.21R, 158 trades) and shorts (+0.21R, 57 trades) both contributed. Dropping the session filter gave +0.19R ±0.15 over 364 trades; limiting the rule to one trade a day cut the profit factor to 1.02 ([our own backtest](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/candidate_backtests.md)).

The published record supports the shape of this rule more than the rule itself. The closest documented system runs the same idea one timeframe up, with a 200-EMA trend filter, a 3-ATR stop and a 2.25R target, and reports profit factors of 1.5 to 1.7 including costs on two data feeds. It is self-reported, its 2025–2026 check period was viewed during development, and its profit leans on a few large winners ([Tuna, MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827)). Applied to our 15-minute rule, those published stop and target settings gave +0.22R ±0.23 with a profit factor of 1.07. The same idea on 5-minute bars fails both independently (−0.19R for a 9-EMA continuation) ([Nambala](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)) and in our own test (−0.06R net, PF 0.94, against +0.11R gross), because a stop one third the size triples cost as a share of risk. **Fifteen minutes is the shortest timeframe on which this rule cleared costs.**

The case against it is substantial and belongs with the result. The edge faded through the sample: +0.34R in the first half of 2025, +0.27R in the second, and **−0.03R with a profit factor of 0.88 in January–May 2026**; monthly results ran from −7.1R to +12.9R. The interval's lower bound is +0.01R after about 45 runs, so a multiple-testing correction would not call it significant. The longer-history check one timeframe up (1-hour bars with a 4-hour trend, 2019 to September 2026) is positive but small, +0.06R ±0.14 with a profit factor of 1.14 over 443 trades and seven positive years in eight. Its controls did about as well (+0.06R with no filter) and all the profit was on the long side (longs +0.15R, shorts −0.10R), which reads as gold's upward drift more than a pullback effect ([our own backtest](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/candidate_backtests.md)). That matters now. Gold fell about 9.5–9.8% in one day on 30 January 2026 from a record near $5,595 ([Lan Tran Hoang, MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778166)) and US trading hours have carried a 28.1% year-to-date decline ([StoneX](https://www.stonex.com/en-gb/news-and-analysis/gold-outlook-asia-buys-the-dip-us-hammers-the-rip-2026-11-16/)), so the rule will be asked to short more often than it was in the sample.

The project's own readiness checklist reaches the same judgment in a stricter form. Saved as a strategy card and run on a $100 account at a fixed 0.1 oz with 20% margin and realistic costs, the rule passes four of nine checks, draws warnings on four and fails one, and one failure is enough for the verdict **"Not ready"** ([our own backtest, addendum](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/candidate_backtests.md)).

| Frequency and size | At most two trades a day, one position at a time, fixed 0.1 oz |

## A tenth of an ounce is the only size $100 can carry

Singapore's retail margin for gold is **20% (5:1), not the 5% that applies to FX**: OANDA Asia Pacific, IG Asia, Phillip Nova and moomoo all list gold at 20% ([OANDA margin rates](https://www.oanda.com/sg-en/legal/margin-rates-retail/); [Phillip Nova margin list](https://www.phillipnova.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/download/MarginList.pdf)). At about $4,050 an ounce, $100 supports at most 0.12 oz, so the standard 0.01-lot minimum of 1 oz ($810 margin) is impossible on a regulated account and a single 0.1-oz position ($81 margin) is the only size that fits. OANDA Asia Pacific lists a gold minimum of 0.1 units on its own platform and 0.001 lot on MT5, but 0.01 lot on MT4 ([OANDA minimum trade size](https://help.oanda.com/sg/en/faqs/minimum-trade-size.htm)). IG's spot gold page shows a 0.05 minimum on a $1-per-point contract, which conflicts with other IG pages ([IG spot gold](https://www.ig.com/sg/commodities/markets-commodities/gold)). Neither sub-ounce minimum has been confirmed on a live account, and that check comes before anything else; the project's OANDA adapter also still sends whole units and has to accept 0.1 ([oanda.py](../gold-backtester/goldlab/live/brokers/oanda.py)). Futures do not fit: Phillip Nova asks US$219 margin for the 1-ounce contract and US$2,180 for the 10-ounce micro. The gold price itself needs re-checking on the day, since a 16 November commentary quotes levels near $3,900, not $4,050 ([StoneX](https://www.stonex.com/en-gb/news-and-analysis/gold-outlook-asia-buys-the-dip-us-hammers-the-rip-2026-11-16/)).

At 0.1 oz every $1 move in gold is $0.10. The backtest's median stop of $15.6 an ounce therefore risks about **$1.56 per trade, 1.6% of the account**; a 2R win pays about $3.10 and costs run to about $0.08 a trade. Size cannot be varied, so risk floats with volatility. By our arithmetic from measured bar ranges, a 2.5-ATR stop is roughly $15 to $20 an ounce at November's volatility and was about $36 in early 2026, which is $3.65 or nearly 4% of the account on one trade. That is above the 1% per trade the risk-of-ruin arithmetic favours, and the only levers are a smaller minimum (IG's 0.05, if it is real) or a rule that skips trades whose stop exceeds a set dollar width, which is an untested change and has to be backtested as a declared variant first. The compensation of 5:1 leverage is that the broker cannot liquidate a 0.1-oz position on any normal move (it would take about $595 an ounce), so the stop order is the entire risk control, though OANDA warns that losses can exceed deposits when prices gap ([OANDA margin rules](https://www.oanda.com/assets/documents/1982/OAP_Margin_Rules_-_July_2025.pdf)). The account can no longer open the trade if the balance falls below about $85 or gold rises above about $4,900 ([our own arithmetic](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/costs_small_account_mechanics.md)).

Offshore accounts remove the margin constraint and add worse ones. At 1 oz on a high-leverage account each dollar of gold is 1% of the balance, so this rule's stop would risk about 16% a trade, and a system with no edge that risks 10% a trade halves its account within 500 trades 94% of the time ([our own simulation](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/costs_small_account_mechanics.md)). MAS and the police say there is "no regulatory safeguard" on unregulated platforms ([MAS, 2018](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2018/cad-and-mas-warn-investors-about-trading-on-unregulated-online-platforms)) and had access to XM and Octa blocked for Singapore residents in June 2025 ([MAS, 2025](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2025/blocking-of-unregulated-overseas-online-trading-platforms)).

What to expect is measured, not estimated. Run as the saved strategy card on a $100 account at a fixed 0.1 oz with 20% margin and realistic costs, the rule **netted +$39.29 over the 16 months (215 trades, 13.4 a month, 40.5% winners, profit factor 1.15)** with a **maximum drawdown of $52.71, 30.5% from the equity peak**. Commission came to $1.51 and overnight financing to $2.71 in total, and no order was rejected for margin. Gold itself rose 71.7% over the same period ([our own backtest, addendum](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/candidate_backtests.md)). That is about $0.18 a trade and roughly $30 a year on $100, and it was not earned evenly: the last 30% of the data ran at −$50 a year against +$70 a year before it. The drawdown is about twice what the R-based figure implies (16R at the median stop is $25), because the losses came in early 2026 when stops were widest and a fixed 0.1 oz cannot shrink to compensate. Streaks will be long: at a 40% win rate, seven or more straight losses is near-certain within 200 trades (91%) and ten or more is likely enough to plan for (38%); the backtest's own worst was eight ([our own arithmetic](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/costs_small_account_mechanics.md)).

On an account this small, the order of the trades matters more than their average. Reshuffling the 215 trades 5,000 times gives a median final balance of $140, a 5th percentile of $58, a 21.6% chance of finishing below $100 and a 95th-percentile drawdown of 65.6%. The historical order was kind, with the profits first, so the balance never approached the roughly $80 to $85 needed to margin a 0.1-oz position. In the unkind orderings it does, and the account can then no longer place the trade at all. For scale on the upside, turning $100 into $10,000 takes 48 unbroken months at 10% a month.

## A verdict needs unseen data first, then 100 live trades

The test has three gates, each with a pass line fixed now. **Gate one is free and decisive: run the saved card on unseen data.** The card already reproduces the 215 trades at a fixed 0.1 oz with 20% margin and financing charged. The project's 5- and 15-minute files end on 3 May 2026, so everything since is untouched by this research: about 85 trades in the lower-volatility correction the method will actually trade in, with more shorts. The checklist's failed item is this same question asked of older data, so the fresh window decides whether 2025 or early 2026 is the better guide. The pass line is a net profit above zero at fixed size with a profit factor above 1, the trend-aligned rule ahead of its no-filter and counter-trend controls, and the result holding at 1.5 times modelled cost. If it fails, the method stays on paper: two losing recent periods in a row would leave a live test nothing to confirm.

Gate two is paper. OANDA's practice account shares the API the project's adapter uses, and four weeks there or in the project's bar replay, a dozen or so trades, checks that a 0.1-unit order is accepted, that the stop and target attach to the entry, that the clock and news filters fire, and that every fill is logged with its spread. Practice fills are idealised, so this gate tests the code and the routine, not the edge. It is also the place to read the broker's real financing charge, since the $2.71 in the backtest rests on the engine's assumed rates of $0.40 an ounce per night long and $0.05 short ([broker.py](../gold-backtester/goldlab/engine/broker.py)).

Gate three is live at 0.1 oz with frozen rules and a stop rule fixed now. The live test ends when any one of three things happens: the balance falls to the margin floor of about $80 to $85, which caps the cost of the experiment near $20 and is not topped up without a fresh decision; the drawdown from any equity peak exceeds the backtest's $52.71; or net expectancy after real costs is below zero at 100 trades. The usual convention of stopping at 1.5 times the backtest drawdown would be $79, which means nothing on a $100 account because the margin floor binds first. The price of so tight a floor is that it sits only ten to thirteen median-sized losses below the start, so an ordinary losing run can end the test before it settles anything. A cash reserve held behind the same 0.1-oz size would buy staying power; a larger position would not, and size does not rise while the checklist says "Not ready".

Three execution guardrails sit on top of the signal. They are proposals from our cost analysis, not sourced standards, and none of them was part of the 215-trade test: no entries from 10 minutes before to 15 minutes after US payrolls, CPI, PCE and Fed decisions (21:30 Singapore time for the 08:30 New York releases in winter), no entry when the live spread exceeds about 1.5 times the session median, and a weekly stop at −6R. One conflict has to be settled at gate one, not ignored. Trades that run seven hours and exit only at stop or target will often sit through the 05:00–08:00 Singapore rollover window, where the median spread on our feed is about $3 an ounce against roughly $0.55 to $0.75 in the main sessions and where stops can fill away from their price ([our own computation](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/costs_small_account_mechanics.md)). A variant that flattens before the New York close should be run as a declared extra and adopted only if it does not destroy the result.

Judge at 100 live trades, about eight months at this pace, and not before unless a stop is hit. Continue if net expectancy after real costs is above zero and the logged cost per round trip is at or below the modelled $0.80 an ounce. Parameters do not change during the test. One hundred trades cannot prove the edge: +0.20R at 40% winners needs about 220 trades to reach a t-statistic of 2, roughly 16 months at this pace, and a 100-trade win rate carries a margin of ±10 points ([our own arithmetic](../research_notes/Gold%20scalping%20method%20for%20small%20accounts/costs_small_account_mechanics.md)). The 100-trade checkpoint says whether live results are consistent with the backtest. The claim that the method works needs the fuller sample and no tuning along the way.

## Conclusion

The research changed the question. We set out to find the best scalping signal and found that the signal matters far less than the ratio of cost to risk: the same pullback rule is negative on 5-minute bars and positive on 15-minute bars for no reason other than stop width, and the breakout rule has a gross edge that a tight stop's cost simply deletes. On a retail gold account, small frequent wins and a positive expectancy pull in opposite directions, because the small target is where the fixed cost bites hardest. The binding constraint is the account, not the indicator. Singapore's 20% margin and a 0.1-oz minimum fix both size and risk, which protects the $100 from liquidation, leaves it about $20 of room before it can no longer place the trade, and rules out compounding in any meaningful way. Growth has to come from adding capital after the method has earned it.

The live spread log is worth more than the trades it accompanies. The two effects with the strongest academic footing, the Asian-hours drift and momentum into the close, fail on our numbers only because a round trip costs about $0.80; an independent test with lower costs found the drift positive after them ([Schuch](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/779464)). If a regulated Singapore account turns out to deliver a verified all-in cost near $0.40, the drift becomes the next hypothesis worth a pre-registered test, with its missing stop as the problem to solve. And if the pullback rule fails on the unseen 2026 data, which is the most likely single outcome for a rule that has already failed our checklist's recent-period test, that result is the finding: there is then no evidence-backed way to day-trade gold on $100, and the honest alternatives are a longer timeframe or no trade.
