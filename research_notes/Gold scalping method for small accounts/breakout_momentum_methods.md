# Breakout, momentum and trend-pullback intraday methods for gold (XAUUSD, 5m/15m): rules, evidence and evidence quality (as of 19 Nov 2026)

**How to read these notes.** Each method has its own section with (i) rules as published, (ii) evidence with numbers, both under
"Cited Findings", and (iii) my assessment of evidence quality under "Inferences" (ratings: high / medium / low / marketing).
Page contents were extracted with an automated page reader, so spot-check any number before quoting it in the final report.
Two PDFs were read directly from extracted text: the Zarattini-Barbon-Aziz ORB paper (Tables 1-3) and the Zarattini-Aziz
conference slides (multi-asset table with the GLD row). "Project data" means the sibling file `own_data_statistics.md`
and the results quoted in the brief; it is not web-sourced. Several sources are MQL5 blog posts by people who sell EAs or
signals; each is labelled. Times: ET = New York, SGT = Singapore (UTC+8).

## Method 1 - New York / COMEX opening range breakout (ORB) and first-hour "initial balance" (IB) break: rules, evidence, failure modes

### Takeaway
The only rigorous ORB evidence is on US stocks and QQQ (Zarattini et al.), where the plain ORB is weak (Sharpe 0.48) and nearly all the edge comes from a relative-volume ("stocks in play") filter, tested with commission only and no out-of-sample period; an independent re-run of the QQQ rule on five index CFDs reproduced the gross edge (about +0.1R per trade, about 22% winners) and found it indistinguishable from zero after spread and slippage. Gold-specific ORB/IB evidence is limited to vendor-tool backtests over 12 months or less with re-fitted parameters (one author told readers to stop trading it in July 2026) and one cost-aware multi-year test that says a gold ORB EA "failed".

### Cited Findings

**Rules as published**
- QQQ/TQQQ paper (Zarattini & Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?", 2023): range = first 5-minute candle of the cash session; direction = direction of that candle; entry at the open of the second 5-minute candle; no trade if the first candle is a doji; stop at the other side of the first candle; target 10x initial risk; otherwise exit at the session close. — [MQL5 blog replication by T. Krueger](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235); paper at [SSRN 4416622](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622)
- "Stocks in Play" paper (Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz, 2024, SSRN 4729284 / SFI 24-98): after the 09:30-09:35 ET candle, place a stop order at the 5-minute high if the first 5 minutes were bullish, or at the 5-minute low if bearish; no order on a doji (open = close); stop loss at 10% of the 14-day ATR from the executed entry; if not stopped, close at 16:00 ET; size so a stop-out loses 1% of capital deployed, leverage capped at 4x. — [paper PDF, Univ. St. Gallen repository](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)
- Same paper, universe and "in play" filter: open price > $5; 14-day average volume >= 1,000,000 shares; 14-day ATR > $0.50; Relative Volume (first-5-minute volume divided by the average first-5-minute volume of the previous 14 days) >= 100%; trade only the top 20 stocks by Relative Volume; commission $0.0035/share; Jan 2016 - Dec 2023; $25,000 start. Only commission is modelled in the text; spread/slippage on the stop entries is not mentioned. — [paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)
- Edgeful "GC initial balance algo" (vendor of a statistics/automation tool; post dated 31 Jan 2026): IB = high/low of 09:30-10:30 ET; 5-minute chart; entry on a 1% retracement after the IB high or low breaks; stop at a 60% retrace back into the IB range; TP1 at 0.25x and TP2 at 0.5x the IB range beyond the broken IB edge; 2 contracts. IB size limits, day filters and costs are not stated. — [Edgeful blog](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/gc-trading-strategy-initial-balance-algo)
- TradeThatSwing "one trade a day gold strategy" (blog; uses the Edgeful algo tool): IB 09:30-10:30 ET, session 09:30-16:00 ET; original rules: IB size 0.3%-1.5% of price, entry in the breakout direction at 25% of the IB distance inside the IB, stop 60% of the IB, target 50% of the IB beyond the IB edge, exit at the close if neither is hit. Re-fitted 22 Feb 2026 (IB 0.4%-2%, entry 10% inside, stop 35%, target 30%, longs only) and again 16 Apr 2026 (IB 0%-2%, entry 25% inside, stop 100% of IB, target 20%, long and short, optionally skip Thursdays). On 13 Jul 2026 the author wrote "avoid until the data (monitor regularly) shows improvement". — [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/)
- CALYX "XAU ORB London-NY overlap M30" (research page, "not for sale"): 5-minute range built from M1 bars starting 13:00 UTC; valid only if the range is 0.20-1.80x M15 ATR(14) and tick activity >= 0.60 of the 20-session median; entry when a completed M30 candle closes >= 0.03 ATR outside the range with body >= 55% of its range and relative tick activity >= 0.80; stop beyond the far side of the range plus 0.05 ATR, setups needing more than 1.50 ATR to the stop rejected; target 0.5R per the current override (another line on the page still says 1R); break-even at +0.5R; flat at 16:00 UTC; one trade per day; skip if spread > 12% of range width. — [CALYX](https://calyx.duckdns.org/eas/xau-orb-london-ny-overlap-m30)
- MNQ (Micro Nasdaq) ORB with trend filter (Substack "Backtests, Not Signals"; range length paywalled): long-only above a ~200-day SMA, short-only below; stop at the opposite side of the opening range capped at 150 ticks; target = risk-reward multiple optimised between 1.4 and 4.2; stop trading after one loss in the session; forced exit at the next session open. — [Backtests, Not Signals](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge)
- Break definition matters: Edgeful's ORB report by default counts a break only when a candle closes beyond the range (stricter than a wick break), and classifies days as breakout, breakdown, double break or no break. — [Edgeful ORB report](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/opening-range-breakout-report)
- COMEX 08:20 ET anchor: I found only commentary (the 08:20 open "frequently produces the session's widest price swings in the first 30-60 minutes") and chart indicators that plot an 08:20 30-minute range; no performance statistics. — [NexusFi gold futures page](https://nexusfi.com/a/instruments/gold-futures-gc); [TradingView "Kane's Gold/Silver ORB" indicator](https://id.tradingview.com/script/7WTYXcAx-Kane-s-Gold-Silver-ORB/) (search-result snippets, pages not opened)

**Evidence: stocks and index products (not gold)**
- Stocks in Play paper, Table 1-2 (2016-2023, net of commission): base 5-minute ORB on all eligible stocks: total return 29%, IRR 3.2%, Sharpe 0.48, hit ratio 41.4%, max drawdown 13%. With Relative Volume >= 100% and top-20 selection: total return 1,637%, IRR 41.6%, volatility 14.8%, Sharpe 2.81, hit ratio 48.4%, max drawdown 12%, alpha 35.8%, beta 0.00. S&P 500 buy-and-hold over the same period: 198%, Sharpe 0.78. — [paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)
- Same paper, effect of the filter: average PnL of 5-minute ORB trades was -0.02R when Relative Volume was below 100%, +0.08R above 100%, and +0.38R above 30x (3,000%). — [paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)
- Same paper, Table 3, opening-range length (all with Relative Volume filter): 5-minute Sharpe 2.81 (1,637%); 15-minute Sharpe 1.43 (272%); 30-minute Sharpe 0.21 (21%, max drawdown 35%); 60-minute Sharpe 0.40 (39%). The authors say the reason the 5-minute range is best "is unclear". — [paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)
- QQQ paper headline: 33.2% vs 14.9% CAGR for buy-and-hold QQQ, Jan 2016 - Feb 2023, 1,795 trades, zero slippage assumed (CXO Advisory summary, search snippet); a critic reports the paper's own figures as a 24% hit rate and +0.13R per trade, with the 33%/yr requiring 4x leverage and no spread/slippage. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy); [MQL5 blog (Krueger)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- Independent re-run of the QQQ rule, unchanged, on NQ, SPX, Dow, DAX and FTSE CFDs, Jan 2015 - Jun 2026 (2,899-2,937 sessions per market). Gross: NQ +0.131R (t 2.9, hit rate 23.2%, 10R reached 2.4% of trades), SPX +0.119R (t 2.4, 20.1%), Dow +0.048R (t 1.1), DAX +0.116R (t 2.4), FTSE +0.050R (t 1.1). Net of spread and slippage (stated round-trip costs NQ 2.5, SPX 0.8, Dow 4.0, DAX 2.5, FTSE 1.5 points, "6 to 12% of the risk on every trade"): NQ +0.002R, SPX -0.081R, Dow -0.081R, DAX -0.038R, FTSE -0.079R. Author's conclusion: "Net of costs no market is distinguishable from zero and four of five are negative." Author sells prop-firm tools; full study on his own site. — [MQL5 blog (Krueger)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- Same re-run, information in the first candle: following the first candle beat a random-direction entry by +0.124R (NQ, t 2.1), +0.128R (DAX, t 2.5), +0.106R (FTSE, t 2.0), +0.092R (SPX, t 1.2), +0.021R (Dow, t 0.4) net; i.e. the direction signal is real in some markets but about the size of the cost. 2015-2017 was negative in every market; 2021-2026 gave "+0.06 R on NQ at best". — [MQL5 blog (Krueger)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- QuantConnect replication of the Stocks in Play rule: Sharpe 2.396 vs 0.836 for SPY, but for 2016 only and with no commissions or slippage; sensitivity: 17 of 25 combinations of range length (5-25 min) and universe size beat the benchmark Sharpe; a forum commenter cites a 17% win rate for the algorithm as written; another reports that stop orders behave differently in minute-resolution backtests and live trading. — [QuantConnect research](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)
- MNQ ORB with ~200-day SMA filter: in-sample 12 Feb 2020 - 30 Dec 2022: 451 trades, win rate 30.4%, profit factor 1.43, Sharpe 1.37, max drawdown 10.9%; out-of-sample 3 Jan 2023 - 27 Feb 2026: 474 trades, win rate 24.9%, profit factor 1.32, Sharpe 1.10, max drawdown 12.8%. Commission $0.68/side; slippage not modelled; buy-and-hold beat it out-of-sample (127.6% vs 94.6%). — [Backtests, Not Signals](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge)

**Evidence: gold-specific**
- Edgeful GC session statistics (six months to Jan 2026): after the 09:30-10:30 ET IB forms, 79.23% of days were single breaks, 13.08% double breaks, 7.69% no break. Algo backtest (TradingView, "last 12 months", 2 contracts, $50k account): +$105,890, win rate 65.76%, profit factor 1.935, max drawdown 11.60%. Trade count, exact dates and commissions/slippage not stated; the post recommends re-optimising "at least monthly". — [Edgeful blog](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/gc-trading-strategy-initial-balance-algo)
- TradeThatSwing results with the same tool: 13 Jan 2025 - 9 Jan 2026, one GC contract on $10,000: "up 411%", trades on 142 of ~250 days, win rate "a little over 50%", average winner about twice the average loser, average loss about $600 (6% of the account), max drawdown about 25%, commissions included, spread/slippage not mentioned. - CALYX XAU ORB (13:00 UTC range): 5 Sep 2025 - 5 Sep 2026, MT5 every-tick, 24 trades, win rate 41.67%, profit factor 2.16, return +5.21% at 1% risk, max drawdown 3.96%, losing streak up to 5; Monte Carlo 5th-percentile return -1.17%; page itself says it "needs forward observation". — [CALYX](https://calyx.duckdns.org/eas/xau-orb-london-ny-overlap-m30)
- "GLM 5.3 improved the Swiss ORB article" (MQL5 blog by E. - Cost-aware multi-strategy test on gold M5 with real bid/ask, 2016-2026, $0.07/oz commission and slippage on stop fills (MQL5 blog by P. No further ORB detail is given. — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- GC weekday effect (vendor statistic): Wednesday is GC's highest double-break day; Monday has the lowest double-break counts across the tickers tested. — [Edgeful "IB by rejection"](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/ib-by-rejection-deep-dive) (search-result snippet)

### Inferences
- **Evidence quality.** Stocks-in-play ORB: medium-high for US stocks with the relative-volume filter (peer-style working paper, 8 years, full statistics), but commission-only costs, no out-of-sample period and a universe-selection mechanism that has no direct equivalent in a single instrument. Index-CFD re-run: medium (transparent method, long sample, explicit costs and a random-direction benchmark, but a self-published blog by a tool seller). Gold IB/ORB (Edgeful, TradeThatSwing, CALYX, Funk): low to marketing: 12 months or less, re-fitted parameters, missing costs, 24-210 trades, and results from the same strongly trending 2025-early 2026 window.
- **Transfer to gold is unproven.** The equity ORB edge is a cross-sectional selection effect (pick the 20 most abnormal stocks out of thousands each day). On one instrument the nearest analogues are (a) trade only days when the first-range tick volume is high relative to its 14-day average for that time of day, and (b) trade only scheduled-news days. Neither has published gold evidence.
- **The "hit ratio" in the Zarattini tables is almost certainly per day, not per trade**: the S&P 500 buy-and-hold row has a hit ratio of 54.9%, which only makes sense as the share of up days. Per-trade win rates for tight-stop ORB are far lower: 17-24% in the QQQ paper, the QuantConnect comment and the index-CFD re-run.
- **Consistency with project data.** The brief's own two-sided COMEX ORB on 5m (PF 1.00 before costs, 0.96 after, 324 trades, 35% wins) and `own_data_statistics.md` (09:30 ET 5-minute range traded in its own direction: +0.08R with a 95% interval of +/-0.32, PF 1.04, 25% winners; 08:20 15-minute range PF 0.81) match the published picture: gross edge near zero to slightly positive, cost of the same size as the edge.
- **Win-rate versus reward profile, frequency, holding time.** Tight-stop ORB held to the close: 17-25% winners, a few large winners, at most one trade a day, holding up to 6.5 hours. Edgeful-style IB with small targets: about 66% winners but reward of roughly 0.44R (TP1) and 0.86R (TP2) against a stop of about 59% of the IB (my arithmetic from the stated levels), so break-even needs about 60% winners and the margin is thin. TradeThatSwing traded 142 of about 250 days, about 2.8 trades a week.
- **Singapore clock.** 09:30 ET is 22:30 SGT in winter (US standard time) and 21:30 SGT in summer; 08:20 ET is 21:20 / 20:20 SGT; a 16:00 ET exit is 05:00 / 04:00 SGT, so a phone trader needs a hard stop and target or an earlier time stop rather than a manual end-of-session exit.
- **Codeable candidate spec for our backtester (my synthesis, to be tested, not a published rule):** range = 09:30-09:35 ET (variants: 09:30-09:45, 09:30-10:30, 08:20-08:35); direction = sign of (range close - range open), skip if zero; entry = stop order at range high (long) or low (short), valid until 12:00 ET, one trade a day; stop = far side of the range, with a cap such as 10-15% of the 14-day daily ATR (skip if the range is wider); exit = 16:00 ET or an earlier time stop (e.g. 3 hours), optional target 2-3R; filters to test one at a time: (1) first-range tick volume / 14-day average for the same window >= 1.0; (2) range width between 0.2 and 1.8 x ATR(14) on 15m; (3) agreement with a higher-timeframe trend (price vs 1-hour EMA 200 or daily SMA 200); (4) skip Wednesdays. Always compare against the same exits with random direction.

### Gaps
- No published multi-year ORB study on gold (spot or GC) with a stated spread/slippage model and a trade list. QuantifiedStrategies' ORB article could not be opened (bot check).
- No statistics for the 08:20 ET COMEX-open range specifically.
- Whether tick volume on spot gold is a usable proxy for the relative-volume filter is untested in any source I found.
- The Nambala post does not give the rules or statistics of the gold ORB EA that "failed".

## Method 2 - London open breakout of the Asian session range (and the "London fake-out then reversal" claim)

### Takeaway
The only multi-year positive gold result is a self-reported EA backtest (profit factor 1.37-1.38 over 715 trades on two brokers, 2023 - Sep 2026) that differs from the naive version in using a wide stop (far side of the range), a range-size filter, a 2.5R target with break-even at 1.5R and a same-day time stop; the more independent tests point the other way (an "Asian box" edge of +0.035R after costs, "too small to trade"; a 2,158-day study in which following the London first hour was a 49% coin flip). The "fake-out then reversal" claim is widely repeated but I found no source that measures its frequency.

### Cited Findings

**Rules as published**
- Generic rule set: mark the Asian session high and low; before London opens place a buy stop just above the high and a sell stop just below the low (small buffer), one-cancels-other; stop at the opposite side of the range (or a few pips beyond). Sources disagree on the window (22:00-07:00 UTC, 00:00-08:00 UTC, or Tokyo open plus the first 2-3 hours), on order time (07:00 or 08:00 GMT) and on targets (1.5-2x the range width versus 30-50% of the range width). — [NYC Servers blog (VPS vendor)](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/london-breakout-strategy); [MQL5 code base entry](https://www.mql5.com/en/code/75586) (search-result snippets; mapping of each variant to each page not verified)
- Gold-specific EA version (MQL5 blog by S. Pinjori, whose EA "Gold London Range Breakout" is in the MQL5 Market): Asian range = high/low from 03:00 to 08:00 broker server time; at 08:00 place buy stop above and sell stop below with a small buffer, OCO; stop at the opposite side of the range, capped at 0.6% of price; take profit 2.5R; move stop to break-even at 1.5R; close everything at 21:00 server time; max one trade a day; skip the day if the range is below 0.30% or above 1.60% of price. — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- "First hour chase" variant tested on gold: if the 08:00-09:00 London-time hour closes above its open, buy at 09:00 (sell if below); stop at the first hour's low/high; close at 13:00 London time if not stopped. — [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)

**Evidence**
- Pinjori EA, MT5 strategy tester on "real ticks", Jan 2023 - Sep 2026, $5,000 start, 1% risk: Broker A net +$6,860, profit factor 1.38, max equity drawdown 17.3%, 715 trades, about 49% winners; Broker B +$6,619, profit factor 1.37, drawdown 18.8%, 716 trades; at 0.5% risk profit factor 1.32, drawdown 11.2%. Settings were chosen on 2023-2025 data; 2026 "out-of-sample": profit factor 1.61 on 157 trades. Spread and commission figures are not stated; no forward results yet. — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- Same source, what hurt (no before/after numbers except one): stops at the middle of the range ("hit by normal noise far too often"); no range filter (narrow ranges give false breakouts, wide ranges mean "the move already happened in Asia"); holding past New York (swap and gap risk, no improvement); 5R targets (profit factor fell to 1.30 and drawdown rose). "The edge is small and needs hundreds of trades to show"; "Drawdowns of 15-19% at 1% risk are normal for this system." — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- Cost-aware test on gold M5, 2016-2026 (real bid/ask, $0.07/oz commission, slippage on stops): an Asian-box breakout made +0.035R per trade, described as "real, but too small to trade"; the author adds "Most intraday ideas are zero before costs. Costs then make them negative" and "Many gold breakouts lost in 2016-2021 and only worked in 2022-2026." — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- London first-hour study, XAUUSD M5 from HFM, 14 Jun 2018 - 6 Nov 2026, 2,158 days, no spread or commission in the main test (author sells a $39 gold signal product): price at 13:00 London was further in the first hour's direction than at 09:00 "only 49.1% of the time"; after an up first hour (1,150 days) price was higher at 13:00 on 51.0% of days versus 52.0% for buying every day; after a down first hour (1,008 days) lower on 46.8% versus 48.0% for selling every day. — [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- Same study, with a stop at the first hour's extreme: buys stopped out on 49.9% of days, closed in profit on 38.4%, average +$0.13 per trade; sells stopped out on 50.7%, in profit on 35.5%, average +$0.48; winners averaged $6.76 (buys) and $8.63 (sells), losers $4.01. The median first-hour spread in the data was $0.20, more than the average buy result. — [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- Same study, range size: follow-through was 50.4% after small first hours (under 0.51x the 20-day average), 50.2% after medium, and 46.6% after large first hours (1.16x or more), identical in both halves of the sample (46.5% and 46.6%); above 50% in only 3 of 9 years (2019, 2022, 2025). — [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- Same study, volatility regime: the median largest adverse move between 09:00 and 13:00 London was $3.8 over the full sample but $13.4 over the last 250 days (from 18 Nov 2025); favourable $3.7 versus $12.6. — [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- Non-gold cross-check: a 10-year London breakout test on EUR/USD reported a 36.26% win rate and profit factor 0.87, concluding the breakout direction carries very little information on its own. — [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/london-breakout-eur-usd) (search-result snippet)
- Vendor listings (marketing): an MQL5 "London Gold Breakout" product claims profit factor 1.36, Sharpe 4.18, win rate 63% on XAUUSD M15 for 2024-2026; a cTrader bot shows profit factor 1.16 with 19.31% drawdown excluding commissions; an H1 Asia-range breakout EA shows profit factor 1.28, win rate 40.30% on 67 trades; a vendor guide cites 60-65% win rates for Asian-range setups from its own backtests. — [MQL5 Market listing](https://www.mql5.com/pt/market/product/176885); [cTrader listing](https://ctrader.com/products/3918); [CALYX Asia breakout](https://calyx.duckdns.org/eas/asia-breakout); [Quantum Algo](https://www.quantum-algo.com/markets/gold/london-session-strategy/) (search-result snippets; attribution of each figure to each listing not verified)
- "Fake-out" claim: several pages assert that the Asian range "often produces false breaks that get sucked back in" and that the London open pushes through the Asian boundary and then reverses; none gives a frequency. — [NYC Servers blog](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/london-breakout-strategy) (search-result snippet)

### Inferences
- **Evidence quality.** Pinjori: low to medium-low. It is the most complete gold-specific breakout backtest I found (3.7 years, two brokers, hundreds of trades, a labelled 2026 hold-out), but it is self-reported by the EA's seller, costs are not stated, and the whole sample sits inside the 2023-2026 bull run. Nambala and Fukushima: medium-low (blog posts without code, but long samples, sensible controls and conclusions that run against the authors' commercial interest). Listings: marketing.
- **Weight of evidence is neutral to negative for the plain version.** Project data agrees: the brief's 15m Asian-range breakout had PF 0.82 before costs and 0.80 after (128 trades), and `own_data_statistics.md` reports that after the first break London closed beyond the level 50% of the time and 66% of breaks closed back inside within three 5-minute bars. A gross profit factor below 1 means the problem is the signal, not the costs.
- **What the "working" variant changes** relative to the naive one: structural stop instead of a tight stop, range band, day-trade holding period with a time stop, modest fixed target with a break-even step. It is a one-trade-a-day day trade (holding up to about 13 hours), not a scalp. Frequency: 715 trades in 3.7 years is about 3.7 a week.
- **Range-size filter direction.** Pinjori skips both very narrow and very wide ranges; Fukushima's data independently supports the "skip wide" half (large first hours followed through least, 46.6%, stable across both halves); the "skip narrow" half has no numbers behind it.
- **Stop distance versus a $100 account.** At $4,050 the Pinjori range band is $12.15-$64.80 and the stop cap is $24.30 (my arithmetic). At 0.01 lot (1 oz, $1 per $1 move per the project's contract assumption) a single stop-out would cost 12-24% of a $100 account. The method needs sub-0.01-lot sizing or a much larger account.
- **Server-time caveat.** "03:00-08:00 server time" is not a named time zone. If the brokers use the common GMT+2/GMT+3 server clock, 08:00 server is about 06:00 London time, i.e. before the 08:00 London open; this is my assumption and should be confirmed before coding. In SGT the London 08:00 open is 16:00 in winter and 15:00 in summer, convenient for Singapore.
- **Codeable spec (Pinjori-style, for replication):** range = high/low of bars from 00:00 to 05:00 UTC (this assumes a GMT+3 server clock; 01:00-06:00 UTC under GMT+2; also test 22:00-07:00 UTC and 00:00-07:00 UTC as alternatives); skip if range/price < 0.30% or > 1.60%; OCO stop entries at range high + buffer and range low - buffer, valid 05:00-12:00 UTC; stop = far side of range, capped at 0.6% of price; target 2.5R; move stop to entry at +1.5R; flat at 18:00 UTC (21:00 server under GMT+3); one trade a day.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how often the Asian high/low is broken in London and then fails ("fake-out" rate); the project's own statistics are the only numbers available.
- Pinjori gives no ablation numbers for the range filter, break-even step or time stop, and no spread/commission figures.
- No evidence on day-of-week, higher-timeframe trend or news-day filters for this setup.

## Method 3 - Break-and-retest entries at session highs/lows, previous-day high/low and round numbers

### Takeaway
I found rule descriptions but no published statistics at all for break-and-retest on gold (or any instrument) with win rate, profit factor and sample size; the closest things are the retracement-entry rules inside the gold IB algos in Method 1, whose results cannot be separated from the rest of those systems.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: when price breaks above or below a major level such as the previous day's high or low, it often pulls back to retest that level before continuing; the trade is taken on the retest in the breakout direction. The vendor's backtest section mentions 11+ years of data but gives no results. — [TradeZella](https://www.tradezella.com/strategies/break-retest) (search-result snippet)
- Retest-style entries embedded in gold IB systems: Edgeful enters on "a 1% retracement" after the 09:30-10:30 ET IB edge breaks; TradeThatSwing enters "25% of the IB distance inside the IB" in the breakout direction (10% in the Feb 2026 refit). — [Edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/gc-trading-strategy-initial-balance-algo); [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/)
- A signal vendor's own study concludes he does not treat the London first hour's direction as a signal and instead waits "for a pullback to a level" before entering, but he does not test that rule. — [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- Related negative result: in the cost-aware gold test, level-based price-action systems lost in every variant (Malaysian support/resistance on H4: -0.10R to -0.23R; an open-source fair-value-gap gold EA: -0.13R per trade on real data against a claimed +48.7% in six months). These are not break-and-retest rules, but they are the only level-based continuation tests with costs that I found. — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)

### Inferences
- **Evidence quality: none (descriptions and marketing only).** Any break-and-retest rule would be a fresh hypothesis.
- **Why it may still be worth one test.** A retest entry uses a limit order instead of a stop order, which removes stop-entry slippage and shortens the stop distance, improving reward per unit of risk; the cost is selection bias: the strongest breakouts never retest, so the trades that fill are disproportionately the ones that fail. Project data (66% of London breaks back inside the Asian range within three 5-minute bars) says retests are common, but says nothing about what happens after them.
- **Codeable spec (my construction):** level L = previous-day high or low, or the Asian or 09:30-10:30 ET range edge; "break" = a 5m or 15m bar closes beyond L by at least 0.1 x ATR(14); "retest" = within the next N bars (N = 6 on 5m) price trades back to within 0.1 x ATR of L and a bar then closes back beyond L in the break direction; enter at that close; stop = 0.5-1.0 x ATR(14) on the other side of L; target 1.5-2R; cancel if a bar closes more than 0.5 x ATR back through L; one trade per level per day. Round numbers: L = nearest multiple of $50 (or $25) at the current price level.

### Gaps
- No backtest or statistics for break-and-retest on gold at any level type; nothing at all on round-number levels.
- No comparison of stop-order breakout entries against retest (limit) entries on the same breakouts.

## Method 4 - Trend-pullback scalps (EMA 9/21, 20/50, 50/200; pullback to fast EMA or VWAP; higher-timeframe trend filter; Supertrend/ADX)

### Takeaway
EMA-pullback rules for gold are everywhere in scripts and vendor pages but no independent 5m/15m backtest with costs supports them; the one cost-aware 5-minute test of an EMA continuation rule on gold lost (-0.19R per trade, 27% winners over 844 trades), while the best-documented positive result is a 1-hour (not 5m/15m) pullback EA with wide 3-ATR stops and a 200-EMA/VWAP trend filter, self-reported at profit factor 1.5-1.7 including costs.

### Cited Findings

**Rules as published**
- Community 5-minute gold scripts: wait for a pullback into the zone between the 9 and 21 EMA, then enter when price crosses back over the signal EMA, with a 50 EMA macro-trend filter; default stop 2.0 x ATR and take-profit 3.0 x ATR. Other pages use fixed distances (stop 50-80 pips, target 100-200 pips or exit when the 9 EMA crosses back), a stop beyond the latest swing low, a 50 EMA + 200 EMA direction requirement, or "trade only in the direction of the 200 EMA slope"; the suggested windows are the London and New York opens. None publishes independent results. — [TradingView "Gold Smart Scalper V3"](https://www.tradingview.com/script/zMAKRynu-Gold-Smart-Scalper-V3-Clean-Chart/); [NYC Servers blog](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/gold-xauusd-trading-strategy); [TradingView "GOLD 5MIN 9/21 EMA"](https://kr.tradingview.com/script/y3P2QEbl-GOLD-5MIN-9-21-EMA-Entry-Arrows-Pro-Setup) (search-result snippets; mapping of each rule to each page not verified)
- "Pullback Sniper X" (MQL5 blog by A. U. Tuna; set files attached, no price stated), pullback core on the XAUUSD H1 chart: trend from a 200 EMA and its slope over 15 bars, plus RSI and the daily VWAP; entry on a pullback to the 40 EMA that also touches an EMA value band or a daily-VWAP anchor, 08:00-14:00 server time; stop 3 x ATR; target 2.25R; stop to break-even +0.1R at 1R; 1.6% risk; up to 5 open trades. The EA also contains three other sub-strategies (range breakout, weekly-open gap, evening drift), and the published results are for the whole EA. — [MQL5 blog (Tuna)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827)

**Evidence**
- Pullback Sniper X, fixed $3,000, spread from data (median $0.08 at entry on the broker feed), $3/lot/side commission, swaps: in-sample 2021-2024: 1,239 trades, profit factor 1.53, max equity drawdown 14.4% (broker data); 1,218 trades, profit factor 1.61 (Dukascopy ticks). Check period Jan 2025 - Nov 2026: 576 trades, profit factor 1.72, drawdown 14.2% (broker); 488 trades, profit factor 1.56, drawdown 15.8% (Dukascopy). Stress tests doubled spread and commission and added $0.30 slippage per trade. — [MQL5 blog (Tuna)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827)
- Same source, author's caveats: the check period was "already viewed during development"; "This set is not to be marketed as robust"; profit is concentrated in a few large winners (without the 10 best trades, check-period net falls from $4,527 to $2,803 on broker data and from $2,834 to $1,263 on Dukascopy); gold's rise helped; Monte Carlo 95th-percentile drawdown 25.0-29.9%; realistic expectation about +$750 a year on $3,000. — [MQL5 blog (Tuna)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827)
- Cost-aware gold M5 test, 2016-2026: the SMB "9 EMA" continuation setup lost -0.19R per trade over 844 trades with a 27% win rate. On daily bars across 12 markets, Linda Raschke's "Holy Grail" (commonly described as an ADX-trend pullback to the 20 EMA; that description is from memory, not from the source) produced 18-40 trades in 25 years as written and -0.20R over 407 trades when relaxed; Rob Hoffman's inventory retracement bar lost -0.27R per trade. — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- Same source: a high-win-rate EA won 76% of trades and still lost, because its stop was 1.2 and its target 0.35, so it "needed 77% just to break even". — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- Marketing-grade claims: a TradingView 5-minute gold scalper (EMA trend plus MACD and session filters) reports profit factor 1.76-2.24, win rate 40-48% and 30-85 signals a month, and states it was developed through 11 backtesting iterations; an MQL5 EMA-pullback EA "targets" profit factor 2.4-3.0 by arithmetic on an assumed 45-55% win rate at 3:1 reward-to-risk, not from a recorded test. — [TradingView script](https://it.tradingview.com/script/Q2PxJ3RB); [MQL5 Market listing](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/170508) (search-result snippets; attribution to these specific listings not verified)
- Recent-window bias warning from an EA seller's own testing: gold's uptrend since the early 2020s makes buy-side logic look strong ("what looked like an edge was mostly the era"); tight-stop breakouts and thin take-profit scalps are punished by intraday reversals; strategies that survive use "daily ranges, wide ATR-based stops"; gold spreads "vary several-fold between brokers and across the day". No numbers are given. — [MQL5 blog (Sakamoto)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/774275)

### Inferences
- **Evidence quality.** 5m/15m EMA pullback scalps: marketing (scripts and listings) with one medium-low negative result (Nambala's 9-EMA test). 1-hour pullback with wide stops (Tuna): low to medium-low: transparent about costs and weaknesses, two data feeds, about 500 trades in the check period, but self-reported, not truly out-of-sample, results blended across four sub-strategies, and dependent on a few large winners in a bull market.
- **Pattern across sources:** the pullback systems that report positive results after costs use higher timeframes (H1), wide volatility-scaled stops (3 x ATR), a long-term trend filter (200 EMA with slope) and a restricted session; the tight 5-minute EMA continuation rule failed. Project data points the same way: `own_data_statistics.md` found EMA20 trend pullbacks on 5m had forward moves smaller than the cost, while buying deep RSI dips in a 1-hour uptrend was the "least-bad family" (PF 1.24 with 2-ATR stop/target, not statistically reliable, long side only).
- **Trade profile.** Tuna's EA: about 26 trades a month in the check period (576 in about 22 months, all sub-strategies), target 2.25R, win rate not reported; low win rate with large winners is implied by the concentration caveat. Holding time is hours, not minutes. On a phone this is feasible only with resting orders and hard stops.
- **Codeable spec to test (my synthesis of the published rules):** trend filter on 1h: close > EMA 200 and EMA 200 higher than 15 bars ago (mirror for shorts); execution on 15m: price pulls back to touch EMA 21 (or session-anchored VWAP) while 15m EMA 9 > EMA 21 > EMA 50 was true before the pullback; trigger = first 15m bar that closes back above EMA 9; stop = 2-3 x ATR(14) below entry or below the pullback low, whichever is further; target 2-2.25R; break-even at 1R; trade only 07:00-16:00 UTC; max 2 trades a day. Variants: ADX(14) > 20-25 or Supertrend(10,3) agreement on 1h as the trend filter instead of the EMA 200.

### Gaps
- No independent, cost-inclusive backtest of EMA 9/21, 20/50 or 50/200 pullback rules on gold 5m or 15m; none with Supertrend or ADX filters; no evidence on VWAP-pullback continuation specifically.
- Tuna's win rate and per-sub-strategy breakdown are not published, so the pullback core's own contribution is unknown.
- No verified live record for any gold trend-pullback scalper (see the costs and track-record section).

## Method 5 - Momentum bursts after scheduled US news (08:30 ET CPI/NFP, FOMC) and "wait for the first pullback" rules

### Takeaway
There is no published, rule-based backtest of post-news momentum on gold; the available numbers are simple event averages showing that gold's 15-minute reaction to an NFP surprise is directionally consistent but small in dollar terms, and that the link between the surprise and the price move fades within about four hours.

### Cited Findings
- FXStreet's recurring NFP studies (about 30 releases per study): gold rose $6.26 on average in the 15 minutes after a weaker-than-expected NFP and fell $4.78 on average after a stronger one; the surprise-reaction relationship weakens by the fourth hour, and a later edition found "no apparent connection" between gold's move and the NFP deviation four hours after the release. — [FXStreet NFP preview (2024)](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-may-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202406061000); [FXStreet NFP preview (2025)](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-january-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202502061000) (search-result snippets; the pages returned HTTP 403 on fetch, so which edition holds which figure is unverified)
- Reversal risk noted in the same series: after the initial reaction, investors "could look to book profits toward the London fix, causing gold to reverse its direction". — [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-february-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-prices-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202303091146) (search-result snippet)
- Academic support for "abnormal days carry more intraday continuation": in gold-ETF (GLD) half-hour data, intraday return predictability is significant at the 1% level when volatility is high and insignificant when it is low, and generally increases with jump size. — [Xu, Bouri, Saeed & Wen, Resources Policy 2020 (text copy)](https://distantreader.org/stacks/cord/cord-0842534-suyml1ad.txt)
- Equity analogue: the ORB edge in US stocks is concentrated in names with abnormal opening volume, which is usually news-driven (-0.02R below 100% relative volume, +0.08R above, +0.38R above 30x). — [Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)
- Session commentary (unmeasured): the 08:00-08:30 ET transition is described on a futures forum as gold's highest-probability window for directional moves. — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?p=910293) (search-result snippet)

### Inferences
- **Evidence quality: low.** Small event samples from a news site, no entry/stop/target rules, no costs. "Wait for the first pullback" rules exist only as discretionary advice; I found no tested version.
- **Costs are worst exactly here.** An average 15-minute NFP move of $5-6 (at pre-2025 price levels) is small against a spread that widens at the release; `own_data_statistics.md` puts median all-in cost at about $0.75-0.89/oz in the New York morning even on ordinary days, and stop orders can slip. A news-burst scalp needs a move many times that.
- **Price-only codeable version (no calendar feed needed beyond a list of dates):** on scheduled-release days only (NFP first Friday, CPI, FOMC), take the 08:30-08:35 ET bar (14:00-14:05 ET for FOMC); require its range >= 1.5 x the 14-day average range of that same bar; wait for the first pullback bar (a bar whose close retraces against the initial bar without closing beyond 50% of it); enter on a break of that pullback bar's extreme in the initial direction, valid for 30 minutes; stop beyond the pullback extreme; target 1.5-2R or exit at 60-90 minutes. Expect about 3-5 trades a month.
- **The useful role of news is probably as a filter, not a standalone method:** a "news day / abnormal first-range volume" flag is the natural gold analogue of the stocks-in-play filter in Method 1, and it can be tested as an on/off condition on the ORB.

### Gaps
- No rule-based post-news momentum backtest on gold with costs; no measured statistics for CPI or FOMC reactions; no tested "first pullback" rule.
- Spread and slippage on XAUUSD in the first minutes after 08:30 ET releases are not quantified in any source I found (another researcher may cover costs).

## Method 6 - Volatility-contraction and channel breakouts (Bollinger/Keltner squeeze, NR4/NR7, Donchian, volatility breakout) on intraday gold

### Takeaway
No published intraday gold backtest of a squeeze or NR4/NR7 breakout exists that I could find; the generic breakout tests that do exist on gold are mostly flat or negative after costs (1-hour Donchian profit factor 0.97; 5-minute volatility breakout 0 to -0.03R; hourly breakout 0.000R over 4,340 trades), with the exceptions being a long-only 1-hour Bollinger breakout in the 2024-2026 bull run (profit factor 1.26) and a paywalled 30-minute futures model with a trend-strength filter (profit factor 1.51 over 23 years).

### Cited Findings

**Rules as published**
- Squeeze definition (TTM Squeeze): the squeeze is "on" when the upper Bollinger Band is below the upper Keltner Channel and the lower Bollinger Band is above the lower Keltner Channel; it "fires" when the Bollinger Bands expand back outside the Keltner Channels; the squeeze gives no direction, which is usually taken from a momentum histogram. Common defaults: 20-period, Bollinger multiplier 2, Keltner multiplier 1.5 x ATR around a 20-period EMA. — [StockCharts ChartSchool](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/ttm-squeeze); [LuxAlgo library](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/ttm-squeeze.md) (search-result snippets)
- Plain Donchian breakout as tested on gold futures: a close above the prior 20-bar high goes long, a close below the prior 20-bar low goes short; market entry on the bar close; fixed 20-point target and 10-point stop; one position at a time; no filters. — [Anup Shinde](https://anupshindedotcom.pages.dev/breakout-continuation-backtested/)
- Gold 1-hour Bollinger breakout (ProRealCode community post, 1 Jul 2026): long only; buy when the close is above the highest high of the prior 53 bars while still below the upper Bollinger Band (15); stop 2.9 x ATR(9); target 5.8 x ATR(9); no trend, volume or time filters. — [ProRealCode](https://www.prorealcode.com/topic/gold-1h-intraday-bollinger-breakout-with-atr-targets-pf-1-26/)
- 30-minute gold futures momentum breakout (Alpha Algo Trading Research, paid newsletter, 29 Jan 2026): long and short mirror rules; when a trend-strength condition permits, a stop order is placed at a breakout level; ATR-based stop; exit when a smoothed open-based average shows the strength condition has faded; all positions flat at 15:00 exchange time; entries allowed overnight. Exact parameters are paywalled. — [Alpha Algo](https://algotr.substack.com/p/from-the-lab-a-robust-gold-strategy)

**Evidence**
- GC 1-hour Donchian-20 breakout, 2020-2024, $100,000, costs not specified: net -$21,453, profit factor 0.97, Sharpe -0.21, max drawdown 58.11%, more than a thousand trades. A regime filter tested separately "helped a little on NQ 1H, dragged on NQ Daily, and could not save GC 1H". The same rule on NQ 1-hour: profit factor 1.08. — [Anup Shinde](https://anupshindedotcom.pages.dev/breakout-continuation-backtested/)
- Cost-aware gold tests, 2016-2026: Larry Williams volatility breakout on gold M5: about 230 trades a year, 0 to -0.03R per trade, every variant lost in 2016-2021; Andrea Unger-style hourly breakout on gold H1: 4,340 trades, 0.000R, "random entries in the same hours did better"; a free gold breakout EA made +0.21R per trade but random bars with the same exits made +0.14R ("Most of its profit was the bull market"). — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- Gold 1-hour Bollinger breakout: 12 Jan 2024 - 24 Apr 2026, 108 trades, profit factor 1.26, Sharpe 0.65, max drawdown 2.00%, time in market 31.1%; costs not stated; no replies or robustness discussion. — [ProRealCode](https://www.prorealcode.com/topic/gold-1h-intraday-bollinger-breakout-with-atr-targets-pf-1-26/)
- GC 30-minute momentum breakout with trend-strength filter: 2002 to about 2025, 1,709 trades, win rate 45%, profit factor 1.51, return/drawdown 15.96, "with slippage and commission" (values not given); 2002-2007 described as untouched out-of-sample; the author says excluding overnight entries "negatively impacts performance". — [Alpha Algo](https://algotr.substack.com/p/from-the-lab-a-robust-gold-strategy)
- Daily-bar comparison: a regime-filtered "structural breakout" on GC, 2007-2026: 312 trades, win rate 39.74%, profit factor 1.72, average win/loss 2.61, max drawdown 20%, costs "$30 + $5 round-trip"; rules paywalled. — [The Rogue Quant](https://roguequant.substack.com/p/id-rather-be-profitable-than-right)
- Squeeze strategies for gold exist as scripts (a 1h/4h squeeze breakout and a "Gold H1 squeeze" with ADX and candle-range filters) but their pages show no performance statistics. — [PineForge](https://getpineforge.com/strategies/xauusd-squeeze-breakout); [TradingView "Gold H1 squeeze"](https://th.tradingview.com/script/JvJc7Wh8-Gold-H1-squeeze/) (search-result snippets)

### Inferences
- **Evidence quality.** Squeeze and NR4/NR7 on intraday gold: none. Generic gold breakouts: medium-low and mostly negative (Shinde, Nambala). ProRealCode: low (long-only, bull-market window, no costs). Alpha Algo: low to medium-low (long sample with costs and an early hold-out, but paywalled rules, vendor-reported, 30-minute futures bars with overnight entries, so it is not a 5m/15m session scalp).
- **Positive breakout results on gold cluster at 30 minutes and above with trend or regime filters and many years of data; at 5 minutes the breakout family is at or below zero after costs.** Project data agrees ("breakout chasing was the worst"; buying a new 1-hour or 4-hour high close had negative forward returns).
- **Codeable spec if the report wants one squeeze test:** on 15m, squeeze on = BB(20, 2) inside KC(20 EMA, 1.5 x ATR(20)) for at least 6 consecutive bars; trigger = first bar that closes outside the Bollinger Band after the squeeze turns off; direction additionally required to match the 1h EMA 200 side; entry at the close; stop = opposite Keltner band or 1.5 x ATR(14); target 2R or exit when price closes back inside the 20-period mean; trade only 07:00-17:00 UTC. NR7 variant (standard definition, not sourced in this pass): a bar whose high-low range is the smallest of the last 7; place OCO stop entries at its high and low for the next bar only.

### Gaps
- No intraday (5m/15m) gold backtest of Bollinger/Keltner squeeze or NR4/NR7 with costs; QuantifiedStrategies' NR7 page could not be opened (bot check), and what I know of it concerns daily bars on stocks/ETFs, not intraday gold.
- No sourced definition of NR4/NR7 was retrieved in this pass (the definition above is the standard one from memory).
- Alpha Algo's and Rogue Quant's parameters are behind paywalls, so neither can be replicated from the public text.

## Method 7 - Time-series intraday momentum (first-hour direction predicts the rest of the session; "noise area" bands, Zarattini-Aziz-Barbon 2024)

### Takeaway
The noise-area intraday momentum rule is well documented on SPY (19.6% net annualised, Sharpe 1.33, max drawdown -25%, 2007-2024, with commission and slippage), but the authors' own multi-asset slide shows it barely works on gold (GLD: 3% return, Sharpe 0.28, 20% drawdown, the worst of nine tickers), and gold-specific studies find only weak, costless half-hour predictability that is not anchored on the first half hour.

### Cited Findings

**Rules as published**
- "Beat the Market" (Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, 2024; SPY, 1-minute data): each minute, upper and lower noise boundaries are set around the day's open using the average move from the open to that minute of the day over the previous 14 trading days (the summary says "average daily return up to that minute"; I read this as the average absolute move); overnight gaps widen the relevant bound; price inside the bounds = no trend. Entries are checked only at HH:00 and HH:30: long if price is above the upper boundary, short if below the lower. Stops: trailing stop at the boundary, or the tighter variant using the higher of the upper boundary and intraday VWAP for longs (lower of lower boundary and VWAP for shorts), executed at the next HH:00/HH:30. Sizing: either 100% of equity, or a 2% daily volatility target with leverage up to 4x. All positions closed at the market close. Costs: $0.0035/share commission plus $0.001/share slippage. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/); slides: [Zarattini & Aziz, MoneyShow 2024 deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Money_Show_Final.pdf)
- Original "market intraday momentum" finding (Gao, Han, Li & Zhou): on the S&P 500 ETF, 1993-2013, the first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return, more strongly on high-volatility and high-volume days. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/) (search-result snippet; pre-2022 source)

**Evidence**
- SPY, May 2007 - April 2024, net of the stated costs: total return 1,985%, 19.6% annualised, Sharpe 1.33, max drawdown -25%; Sharpe 3.50 when VIX is above 40. — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-sp500-etf-spy/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/); [QuantifiedStrategies Substack](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/systematic-intraday-trend-following)
- The published track is uneven by year: the slide deck's calendar table shows -12.8% in 2016 and -6.9% in 2017 against +63.4% in 2008 and +61.1% in 2018. — [MoneyShow 2024 deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Money_Show_Final.pdf)
- "Same trading rules" applied to other tickers (authors' slide; period and costs not stated in the extracted text): SPY IRR 30% / Sharpe 2.11 / max drawdown 9%; QQQ 28% / 2.03 / 9%; BITO 24% / 1.72 / 7%; SOXX 18% / 1.48 / 9%; TSLA 31% / 2.19 / 14%; NVDA 15% / 1.09 / 11%; AMD 17% / 1.11 / 19%; AAPL 11% / 0.77 / 17%; **GLD 3% / Sharpe 0.28 / max drawdown 20%** (volatility 11%). — [MoneyShow 2024 deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Money_Show_Final.pdf)
- QuantifiedStrategies' write-up states "We have not conducted our own backtests of this strategy" and warns that slippage "is not accounted for" and "can be significant". — [QuantifiedStrategies Substack](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/systematic-intraday-trend-following)
- Gold ETF half-hour predictability (Xu, Bouri, Saeed & Wen, Resources Policy 2020; GLD 1-minute data aggregated to 13 half-hours, sample ending May 2020): for GLD the significant predictor of the last half-hour is the fifth half-hour return (t = 3.03, in-sample R-squared 0.49%, out-of-sample R-squared 0.26%); predictability is significant in high-volatility periods and insignificant in low-volatility periods; transaction costs are not mentioned in the text retrieved. — [text copy of the paper](https://distantreader.org/stacks/cord/cord-0842534-suyml1ad.txt)
- Chinese metals futures: before night trading existed, the daytime first half-hour return predicted later half-hour returns but not necessarily the last one; after night sessions began, the daytime first half-hour lost its predictive power. — [Zhou, working paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/686816/4a-Z-Ivy-Zhou.pdf) (search-result snippet)
- Direct gold test of "first hour predicts the session": over 2,158 days, the direction of the 08:00-09:00 London hour was followed through to 13:00 on 49.1% of days and to 16:00 on 52.2% (up hours) and 50.2% (down hours), against 51.1% and 48.9% for always buying or always selling. — [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- I found no published application of the noise-area rule to gold futures or spot gold; public re-implementations target SPY and NQ. — [TradingView noise-area scripts](https://fr.tradingview.com/scripts/noise/) (search-result snippet)

### Inferences
- **Evidence quality.** SPY: medium-high (academic working paper, 17 years, explicit costs, public code), though in-sample and uneven by year. Gold: the best available evidence is negative or negligible: the authors' own GLD row (low-medium quality as a slide, but it is evidence against the authors' interest), tiny R-squared values without costs (Xu et al.), and a coin-flip result for the London first hour (Fukushima). Rating for gold: low, leaning negative.
- **Why it probably does not transfer.** The SPY effect is attributed to intraday demand/supply imbalances that persist into the close (the paper analyses dealer gamma), anchored on a single cash-session open and close. Spot gold trades almost 24 hours with three session opens and no closing auction, and the one commodity study with a session-structure change found the first-half-hour effect disappeared once a night session existed.
- **Project data agrees:** `own_data_statistics.md` reports that the first 30 or 60 minutes of New York predicted the rest of the morning 49-53% of the time, morning versus afternoon 52%, and 5-minute variance ratios of 0.90-1.03.
- **Codeable spec if the report still wants to test it (cheap to run, low prior):** anchor = 08:20 ET or 09:30 ET open; for each 5m bar time t compute sigma(t) = 14-day average of |close(t)/anchor open - 1|; upper = max(open, previous close) x (1 + sigma(t)), lower = min(open, previous close) x (1 - sigma(t)); every 30 minutes, go long if close > upper, short if close < lower; exit when price closes back through max(upper, session VWAP) for longs (mirror for shorts) or at 16:00 ET; one position at a time.

### Gaps
- No published noise-area or first-hour-momentum backtest on GC or XAUUSD with costs; the GLD row's test period and cost assumptions are not stated in the slide text.
- The Xu et al. excerpt does not give GLD timing-strategy returns or say explicitly that the first half-hour is insignificant for GLD (only that the fifth is the significant predictor).

## Which refinements have evidence behind them (direction filters, volatility and range filters, retest entries, time stops, partial exits, day of week)?

### Takeaway
The refinement with the strongest measured effect is an abnormal-activity ("in play") filter, but it is proven only for US stocks; next are trading in the direction of the opening candle (worth about +0.10-0.13R versus random direction on some index CFDs, roughly the size of costs) and skipping oversized ranges (one 2,158-day gold study); wide structural stops, same-day time stops and break-even steps are supported only by vendor statements without numbers, and retest entries, partial exits and day-of-week rules have no usable evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Abnormal-activity filter (strongest, stocks only):** ORB average PnL -0.02R when first-5-minute relative volume < 100%, +0.08R above 100%, +0.38R above 30x; portfolio Sharpe 0.48 without the filter versus 2.81 with it. — [Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)
- **Direction of the opening candle:** beats random direction by +0.124R (NQ), +0.128R (DAX), +0.106R (FTSE) net, not significant on SPX or Dow; on gold, an SMC "first candle" rule on M5 did no better than a coin flip (-0.20R, 1,454 trades) and the London first hour's direction followed through 49.1% of the time. — [MQL5 blog (Krueger)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235); [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404); [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- **Long-term trend/regime filter:** an MNQ ORB restricted to the side of a ~200-day SMA held up out-of-sample (profit factor 1.32, 474 trades, slippage not modelled); a regime filter "could not save" a GC 1-hour Donchian breakout; an ML trade filter on gold and indices had out-of-sample AUC 0.49-0.54 (no skill). — [Backtests, Not Signals](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge); [Anup Shinde](https://anupshindedotcom.pages.dev/breakout-continuation-backtested/); [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- **Range-size band:** used by every gold breakout system that reports positive results (0.30-1.60% of price; 0.20-1.80 x M15 ATR; IB 0.3-1.5% of price), but only one dataset measures it: follow-through after large London first hours (>= 1.16x the 20-day average) was 46.6% versus about 50% for small and medium, stable in both sample halves. — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [CALYX](https://calyx.duckdns.org/eas/xau-orb-london-ny-overlap-m30); [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/); [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- **Opening-range length:** in stocks, 5-minute ranges far outperformed 15-, 30- and 60-minute ranges (Sharpe 2.81 / 1.43 / 0.21 / 0.40). — [Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)
- **Stop width:** for gold, mid-range stops "were hit by normal noise far too often" and tight-stop breakouts are "punished" by intraday reversals (vendor statements, no numbers); in stocks the opposite design (very tight stop at 10% of daily ATR, about 17-24% winners) is what the positive results use. — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [MQL5 blog (Sakamoto)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/774275); [QuantConnect research](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)
- **Target size:** on the gold London breakout, 2.5R beat 5R (profit factor fell to 1.30 and drawdown rose at 5R); on MNQ, optimising for win rate pushed it above 40% but lowered the Sharpe ratio. — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [Backtests, Not Signals](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge)
- **Time stops:** closing before the end of the New York session beat holding overnight on the gold London breakout (qualitative); the academic ORB and noise-area rules both exit at the session close; a 30-minute GC model flattens at 15:00 exchange time. — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content); [Alpha Algo](https://algotr.substack.com/p/from-the-lab-a-robust-gold-strategy)
- **Break-even step and partial exits:** present in the gold systems (break-even at 1.5R; break-even +0.1R at 1R; TP1/TP2 at 0.25x/0.5x of the IB) with no ablation showing they help. — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [MQL5 blog (Tuna)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827); [Edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/gc-trading-strategy-initial-balance-algo)
- **Day of week:** only vendor statistics (GC double breaks peak on Wednesday) and an optional "exclude Thursdays" in a re-fitted rule set. — [Edgeful "IB by rejection"](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/ib-by-rejection-deep-dive); [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/)
- **Volatility regime:** intraday continuation is stronger in high-volatility states (SPY noise-area Sharpe 3.50 when VIX > 40; GLD half-hour predictability significant only in high-volatility periods). — [QuantifiedStrategies Substack](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/systematic-intraday-trend-following); [Xu et al. text copy](https://distantreader.org/stacks/cord/cord-0842534-suyml1ad.txt)
- **Testing controls that separated real from spurious results:** variants written down before the run (at most 24 per test), both halves of the sample checked, random-entry benchmark with identical stops and exits, t-statistic threshold of 3.3 rather than 2. — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)

### Inferences
- Ranking of refinements by evidence for gold specifically: (1) skip oversized opening/Asian ranges (one independent-ish dataset plus universal use by vendors); (2) wide, structure-based stops with a same-day time stop (vendor consensus, no numbers, consistent with project cost data); (3) abnormal-activity filter (strong mechanism and stock evidence, untested on gold); (4) opening-candle direction (real on indices, coin flip on the two gold tests found); (5) higher-timeframe trend filter (mixed: helped MNQ ORB, did not rescue a gold 1-hour breakout); (6) retest entries, partial exits, day-of-week (no evidence).
- Because the gross edge in every credible test is about 0.1R or less, any refinement that shortens the stop (tight stops, retest entries) raises cost as a share of risk and can erase its own benefit; refinements that cut the number of trades without shortening the stop (range band, activity filter, one trade a day) are the safer ones to test.
- High-volatility conditioning cuts both ways in 2025-2026: continuation effects are reported to be stronger, but the dollar stop needed has roughly tripled (median adverse excursion $13.4 versus $3.8), which matters for a $100 account.

### Gaps
- No ablation table for any gold system (filter on versus off with trade counts); every gold "refinement" claim is either qualitative or confounded with the rest of the system.
- No evidence on news-day-only or no-news-day filters for gold breakouts.

## Costs, sample quality, verified live track records, and practicality on a phone with $100

### Takeaway
Across this family the credible gross edge is about 0.1R per trade or less and realistic costs of 6-12% of risk are enough to remove it; almost all gold-specific positive results come from 2023-2026 backtests by people selling the strategy or a tool, and I found no verified live gold breakout or pullback account with a long history and no martingale.

### Cited Findings
- Cost versus edge: in the index-CFD ORB re-run, spread and slippage cost "6 to 12% of the risk on every trade" and turned +0.05 to +0.13R gross into -0.08 to +0.002R net; "a 2.5-point round trip on a 25-point stop costs roughly that much". — [MQL5 blog (Krueger)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- With real bid/ask, $0.07/oz commission and stop slippage on gold M5 (2016-2026), "Most intraday ideas are zero before costs. Costs then make them negative"; no strategy out of 400+ variants passed all tests. — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- Bull-market contamination: gold rose "from about $1,060 to $4,260" over that data; "Many gold breakouts lost in 2016-2021 and only worked in 2022-2026"; a free breakout EA's +0.21R was mostly matched by random entries (+0.14R). — [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- Cost assumptions in the headline studies: Stocks in Play ORB: commission only; QQQ ORB: zero slippage; QuantConnect replication: no costs; MNQ ORB: commission only; Edgeful/TradeThatSwing: commissions at most, no spread/slippage; Pinjori: not stated; ProRealCode: not stated; Tuna: spread from data plus $3/lot/side, with doubled-cost and +$0.30 slippage stress tests; Beat the Market: $0.0035 commission + $0.001 slippage per share. — [paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy); [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/); [Backtests, Not Signals](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge); [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/); [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [MQL5 blog (Tuna)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827); [CXO Advisory (noise area)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/)
- Spread reference points from the sources: median first-hour (08:00-09:00 London) XAUUSD spread $0.20 on HFM data; median spread at entry $0.08 on one raw-spread broker feed. — [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305); [MQL5 blog (Tuna)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827)
- Execution failure modes reported: stop orders fill differently live than in minute-bar backtests; an EA sized trades "about 10 times too large" on a second broker because the tick value did not match the real gold contract; session rules depend on broker server time. — [QuantConnect research (comments)](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/); [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- Regime decay is fast: the gold IB break system was re-fitted in Feb and Apr 2026 and shelved in Jul 2026 when single breaks fell to "nearly 50/50"; Edgeful itself advises re-optimising "at least monthly". — [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/); [Edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/gc-trading-strategy-initial-balance-algo)
- Drawdowns in the positive gold backtests: 15-19% at 1% risk "are normal" (London breakout); about 25% and a $600 average loss per GC contract on a $10,000 account (IB break); Monte Carlo 95th-percentile drawdown 25-30% (H1 pullback EA). — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/); [MQL5 blog (Tuna)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827)
- Live track records: my search for Myfxbook-verified XAUUSD breakout/scalper accounts with long history and no martingale found none that qualify. Examples of what exists: a demo account public since 25 Aug 2026 showing +13.86%, 3.76% drawdown and 94 trades by 4 Sep 2026; a vendor thread headlined "152% in under 1 month"; MQL5 signals with 3 trades or negative growth. — [MQL5 blog (GOLD KING v6 forward test)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/775306); [Forex Peace Army thread (Hunter ProGold)](https://www.forexpeacearmy.com/community/threads/hunter-progold-expert-advisor-myfxbook-verified-performance-152-in-under-1-month.87557/latest); [MQL5 signal](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/2380262) (search-result snippets)

### Inferences
- **Cost as a share of risk for the published stop sizes (my arithmetic).** Using the brief's cost model (spread $0.20-0.40 + $0.07 commission + $0.05 slippage, about $0.32-0.52/oz round trip): a $2 stop costs 16-26% of R, a $5 stop 6-10%, a $7.50 stop (10% of a $75 daily ATR) 4-7%, a $15-25 structural stop 1-3%. Using the Dukascopy-based median all-in cost of about $0.75-0.80/oz in `own_data_statistics.md`, those shares roughly double. Since the credible gross edge is about 0.1R, stops below roughly $5-8 are structurally unprofitable for continuation entries; this is the main reason the tight 5-minute versions fail and the wide-stop, hold-for-hours versions are the only ones reporting profits.
- **That collides with the $100 account.** At 0.01 lot (1 oz) a $7.50 stop is 7.5% of the account and a $15-25 structural stop is 15-25%. Risking 1-2% per trade on $100 allows a stop of only $1-2 at 0.01 lot, exactly the zone where costs eat the edge. A continuation method from this family is therefore only testable with real money on $100 if the broker allows sizes below 0.01 lot (or a cent account); otherwise the test trade is closer to a 5-10% bet per trade.
- **Evidence-quality summary by source type:** academic working papers (Zarattini et al.): medium-high but not on gold; cost-aware independent-style blog studies (Krueger, Nambala, Fukushima): medium to medium-low and mostly negative; vendor/self-reported gold backtests (Pinjori, Tuna, Alpha Algo, Rogue Quant): low to medium-low; tool-vendor statistics and listings (Edgeful, TradeThatSwing, CALYX, Funk, TradingView/MQL5 listings): low to marketing; verified live records: none found.
- **Phone practicality.** The methods that survive best are one-trade-a-day, bracket-order designs with fixed clock times: London range orders at 15:00/16:00 SGT, New York opening-range orders at 21:30/22:30 SGT. They need two pending stop orders with attached stop-loss and take-profit, which is workable on a phone; what is not workable manually is a session-end exit at 04:00-05:00 SGT, so the test design should use a hard target/stop plus a time stop that falls before the trader's bedtime, and should measure how much that earlier exit costs.
- **Sample warning for our own backtest.** Jan 2025 - May 2026 is 16 months inside the exact regime that flatters long-side breakouts and trend rules. Published half-sample checks (2016-2021 versus 2022-2026) flipped the sign of many gold breakout results, so a pass on our window is weak evidence; the Nambala controls (pre-registered variants, random-direction benchmark, both halves) are cheap to copy.

### Gaps
- No verified live account evidence for any method in this family on gold.
- No source measures XAUUSD spread widening at the London open, 08:20/09:30 ET or 08:30 ET news releases in a way that can be reused; the project's Dukascopy spreads are the best available.
- Minimum trade size below 0.01 lot at the trader's broker is unknown and decides whether sensible risk per trade is possible on $100.

## Which single method from this family is the most defensible candidate to backtest?

### Takeaway
None of the continuation methods has good gold-specific evidence after costs, so the honest prior is a profit factor of about 1.0-1.15 at best; if one must be picked from this family, the most defensible is a one-trade-a-day New York opening-range (or first-hour) breakout with filters that cut trades without shortening the stop, because it is the only family member with rigorous (non-gold) research behind its refinements and its unfiltered gross result on our own data is about break-even rather than negative, with a higher-timeframe trend-pullback on 15m/1h as the runner-up.

### Cited Findings
- Refinements with measured support for ORB: activity filter (-0.02R to +0.08R to +0.38R), opening-candle direction (+0.10-0.13R versus random on NQ/DAX/FTSE), shorter range (5-minute Sharpe 2.81 versus 0.21-0.40 for 30-60 minutes), long-term trend side (MNQ profit factor 1.32 out-of-sample). — [Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz paper PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content); [MQL5 blog (Krueger)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235); [Backtests, Not Signals](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge)
- The counter-case: the same rule nets to zero on five index CFDs after costs, a gold ORB EA failed a cost-aware 10-year test, and the gold IB break system stopped working by mid-2026. — [MQL5 blog (Krueger)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235); [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404); [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/one-trade-a-day-gold-strategy-411-in-last-year-fully-automatable/)
- London breakout: positive only in a seller's backtest (profit factor 1.37-1.38); independent-style tests show +0.035R ("too small to trade") and a 49.1% follow-through rate. — [MQL5 blog (Pinjori)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404); [MQL5 blog (Fukushima)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780305)
- Trend pullback: positive only at H1 with 3-ATR stops in a self-reported test (profit factor 1.56-1.72 in 2025-2026); the 5-minute 9-EMA continuation lost -0.19R. — [MQL5 blog (Tuna)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778827); [MQL5 blog (Nambala)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777404)
- Noise-area momentum: GLD Sharpe 0.28 in the authors' own multi-asset table. — [MoneyShow 2024 deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Money_Show_Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Ranking within this family (my judgment):** (1) filtered New York ORB / first-hour break; (2) higher-timeframe trend pullback with wide ATR stops; (3) Pinjori-style London range breakout; (4) squeeze / channel breakouts; (5) post-news momentum as a standalone; (6) noise-area / first-hour momentum; (7) break-and-retest (no evidence either way, could be tested as an entry variant of 1 or 3).
- **Why New York ORB over London breakout:** on the brief's own numbers the unfiltered New York ORB is PF 1.00 gross and 0.96 net (costs are the gap, and a filter with any real information can close it), whereas the unfiltered London breakout is PF 0.82 gross (the signal itself is wrong-signed, which argues for the other researcher's fade family rather than for filtering). The New York window is also where the academic refinements were developed and where gold's activity filter (relative tick volume, scheduled news) is most meaningful.
- **Proposed pre-registered test for candidate 1 (keep to 12 or fewer variants):** base = 09:30 ET range, entry by stop order in the direction of the range candle only, stop at the far side of the range (skip if wider than a set fraction of daily ATR), exit at a time stop (2-3 hours) or 2R, one trade a day. Factors: range length {5, 15 minutes}; activity filter {off, first-range tick volume >= 1.0x its 14-day average}; trend filter {off, same side as 1h EMA 200}. Report for each: trades, win rate, average R with a confidence interval, profit factor before and after costs, the same statistics by half-sample, and the difference versus random direction with identical exits. Decision rule: only proceed to test-trading if net average R is positive in both halves and beats random direction.
- **Honest expectation.** Given project statistics (no 5-minute rule with a reliable edge after costs; "breakout chasing was the worst") and the published record, the likeliest outcome is that the filtered ORB is statistically indistinguishable from zero on 16 months of data. The report should weigh this family against the reversal/pullback family before committing, and treat any $100 live test as a process test at the smallest possible size, not as income.
- **If the report prefers the runner-up (trend pullback):** use the 15m/1h spec in Method 4; it trades less often, uses wide stops (costs 1-3% of R) and is aligned with the "least-bad family" in project data, but it has no rigorous published support and its only positive evidence is long-biased in a bull market.

### Gaps
- No method in this family has an out-of-sample, cost-inclusive, gold-specific result from a source without a commercial interest; the choice therefore rests on our own backtest, which has a short and regime-specific sample.
- Whether a tick-volume activity filter carries any information on spot gold is the key unknown for candidate 1 and can only be answered by running it.
