# Academic and quantitative evidence on intraday gold return patterns and retail day-trading profitability

Notes compiled 19 November 2026. Source-type tags used throughout: **[PR]** peer-reviewed journal article, **[WP]** working paper or preprint, **[THESIS]** student thesis, **[REG]** regulator publication, **[PRAC]** practitioner blog or vendor material (not peer-reviewed). "Gross" means before transaction costs. Where I could only read an abstract or a secondary summary, the entry says so. Arithmetic that converts published coefficients into dollars at a $4,050 gold price is mine and sits under Inferences.

## 1. Intraday momentum: does the "first half-hour predicts last half-hour" effect, or intraday time-series momentum, exist in gold or commodity futures?

### Takeaway
Yes, statistically: the best study (Baltussen et al., JFE 2021) finds that COMEX gold's return up to the last half-hour of the old pit session positively predicts the last half-hour (13:00-13:30 ET), over 1984-2020. The effect is tiny (R² about 0.25%, roughly 1 basis point of drift per 1% prior move), was measured before costs, and the authors themselves say it may not be exploitable after costs.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] Original equity result.** Gao, Han, Li and Zhou (JFE 2018) found on S&P 500 ETF data 1993-2013 that the first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return. This is an equity-index result, not gold. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)
- **[PR] Cross-asset extension that includes COMEX gold.** Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens, "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum" (JFE 2021, 142:377-403), use one-minute data on 60+ futures, December 1974 to May 2020, including 21 commodity futures (5 metals, 4 energies, 12 softs). The return from the previous close to 30 minutes before the close (rROD) positively predicts the last 30 minutes (rLH) in every asset class. — [Full text PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403)
- **[PR] Gold-specific coefficients (Table B3, contract GC).** Sample 5 January 1984 to 1 May 2020, 9,044 trading days, trading day defined as 08:20-13:30 ET (matched to volume patterns, i.e. the old pit session). Regressing the last half-hour return on the overnight-plus-first-half-hour return: slope 1.43 (x100), t = 2.98, R² 0.23%, out-of-sample R² 0.18%. Regressing it on the whole rest-of-day return: slope 1.09 (x100), t = 2.95, R² 0.25%, out-of-sample R² 0.08%. The middle-of-day and second-to-last half-hour returns are not significant for gold. — [Baltussen et al., Table B3 and Table A3](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **[PR] Metals group and all commodities, pooled.** Metals: slope on rest-of-day return 1.19 (x100), t = 4.50, R² 0.26%, out-of-sample R² 0.31%. All 21 commodities: slope 1.21 (x100), t = 3.63, R² 0.15%. For comparison, equity index futures: slope 4.18 (x100), R² 2.45%. So the commodity slope is under a third of the equity slope, with about one-sixteenth the R². — [Baltussen et al., Tables 3 and B3](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **[PR] Sub-period stability.** Commodity slope on rest-of-day return was 1.59 (x100), R² 0.23% in 1974-1999 and 1.04 (x100), t = 2.86, R² 0.12% in 2000-2020. The effect roughly halved in explanatory power in the electronic era but remained significant. — [Baltussen et al., Table 5](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **[PR] Strategy results, gross.** A 1/N commodity-futures portfolio that goes long in the last half-hour when the rest-of-day return is positive and short otherwise earned 4.34% a year with 3.05% volatility, Sharpe 1.42, success rate 56%. The first-half-hour-only signal earned 2.48% a year, Sharpe 0.82, success rate 54%. "Always long the last half-hour" earned -0.68% a year. — [Baltussen et al., Table 6](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **[PR] Costs were not included.** The authors write "Note that we do not consider transaction costs" and add that the strategy "might not be exploitable to many investors after accounting for transaction costs". As an example of where it may still work, they mention a positive net Sharpe ratio for S&P 500 futures at a cost of one tick; they give no such figure for gold or commodities. — [Baltussen et al., section 3](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **[PR] Mechanism and decay.** The authors attribute the effect to gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged-ETF rebalancing near the close, and report that the last-half-hour move reverts over the following days (price pressure, not information). — [Baltussen et al., abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403)
- **[PR] Gold ETF evidence is different in shape.** Xu, Bouri, Saeed and Wen (Resources Policy 2020) split the 09:30-16:00 ET session of USO, GLD and SLV into 13 half-hours using one-minute data. For GLD the significant predictor of the last half-hour is the fifth half-hour (11:30-12:00 ET by my count from a 09:30 start), not the first, with t = 3.03, in-sample R² 0.49% and out-of-sample R² 0.26%. For USO (crude oil) the first half-hour is the predictor. The text retrieved does not mention transaction costs. — [Xu et al. full text](https://distantreader.org/stacks/cord/cord-0842534-suyml1ad.txt)
- **[PR] Chinese gold and silver futures.** After night trading was introduced in Shanghai, the day session's first half-hour lost its predictive power and the night session's first half-hour (which overlaps London and New York hours) became the predictor; momentum was stronger with higher volatility and lower liquidity. Abstract and search-summary level only. — [Ma, Bouri, Xu, Zhou, Global Finance Journal 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/glofin/v64y2025ics1044028325000110.html); [earlier working-paper version](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/686816/4a-Z-Ivy-Zhou.pdf)
- **[PR] Other Chinese commodity futures (supporting, not gold).** Jin, Kearney, Li and Yang (Journal of Futures Markets 2020) find the first half-hour predicts the last half-hour in copper, steel rebar, soybean and soybean meal futures, most strongly on high-volume or high-volatility openings in metals. Cost treatment not confirmed. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v40y2020i4p632-650.html)
- **[PR] Volatility, not direction, is the more predictable quantity.** For Chinese gold futures, realised volatility earlier in the day predicts last-half-hour realised volatility, and US gold futures information helps. — [Ye, Lv, Xue, Luo, Finance Research Letters 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v58y2023ipas154461232300394x.html)

### Inferences
- Converting the gold coefficient to dollars: a slope of 1.09 (x100) means a 1% move from the prior close to 13:00 ET (about $40 at $4,050) predicts about 1.1 basis points, or roughly $0.44 an ounce, of further drift in the next 30 minutes. A full one-ATR day (about $75, 1.85%) predicts about $0.82. Our fixed costs before spread are about $0.12-0.17 an ounce ($0.07 commission equivalent plus $0.05-0.10 slippage), and the bid/ask spread comes on top. The predicted drift is the same order of magnitude as the round-trip cost, so the net edge is close to zero for a retail account.
- The pooled commodity strategy's 4.34% a year over roughly 250 trades is about 1.7 basis points gross per trade. That is a diversified 21-market average; a single market such as gold will be noisier.
- The signal is defined on the pit close at 13:30 ET. In Singapore that window is about 02:00-02:30 (US winter time) or 01:00-01:30 (US summer time), which is impractical to trade by hand.
- The effect was estimated on a session definition that ended in 2020. Whether 13:30 ET is still the relevant "close" for gold in 2025-2026, when most volume is electronic, is untested in the literature I found.
- The gold evidence is not consistent about which half-hour matters (overnight plus first half-hour in futures, 11:30-12:00 ET in GLD, night-session open in Shanghai). That inconsistency is what a weak, sample-dependent effect looks like, and it argues against building a method around a specific predictor half-hour.

### Gaps
- No study found that tests intraday momentum on spot XAUUSD or on GC futures after 2020, or under the 2025-2026 volatility regime.
- No gold-specific after-cost result exists in the peer-reviewed literature I could access. Baltussen et al. exclude costs; Xu et al. do not mention them in the retrieved text.
- I could not open the Hull repository copy of Jin et al. or the AUT working paper (HTTP 403), so their cost treatment and coefficient sizes are unverified.
- Intraday time-series momentum at shorter horizons (for example the last 30 minutes predicting the next 30 minutes on a rolling basis) has no gold-specific study that I found.

## 2. Opening range breakout: what do the studies find, what makes it work, and is there gold-specific evidence?

### Takeaway
The published ORB evidence is for US equities and for crude oil and S&P 500 futures, and it works mainly in high-volatility or high-relative-volume conditions; the headline equity papers assume no spread or slippage, and an independent CFD replication found the gross edge is real but about the size of the spread. I found no peer-reviewed or working-paper ORB study on gold.

### Cited Findings
- **[WP] Zarattini and Aziz (2023), "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?"** Tests a 5-minute ORB on QQQ and TQQQ over 2016-2023. The authors report a total return of 1,484% versus 169% for passive QQQ and an annualised alpha of 33% net of commissions. Not peer-reviewed. — [Concretum summary by the authors](https://concretumgroup.com/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622)
- **[PRAC, secondary summary] Assumptions in that paper.** $25,000 starting capital, up to 4x leverage, $0.0005 per share commission, 1% risk per trade, exit at the close, 1,795 trades from January 2016 to February 2023, and no bid-ask spread and no slippage. The same summary gives 33.2% versus 14.9% CAGR for the QQQ version. I did not verify these against the paper's own tables. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy)
- **[WP] Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz (2024), "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy for the U.S. Equity Market".** Over 7,000 US stocks, 2016-2023. Restricting a 5-minute ORB to the top 20 "Stocks in Play" each day gives a net return above 1,600%, Sharpe 2.81 and annualised alpha 36%, versus 198% for the S&P 500. "In play" is defined by relative volume: volume in the first 5 minutes divided by the 14-day average for the same window. Stop-loss is 10% of ATR and the exit is at the close. Swiss Finance Institute research paper, not a journal article. — [Concretum summary](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/); [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html)
- **[PRAC] Independent replication of the Stocks in Play paper.** QuantConnect reproduced it on a smaller universe and got a Sharpe ratio of 2.396 against 0.836 for SPY, with beta -0.042, and Sharpe above 2 for every universe size tried. — [QuantConnect research](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)
- **[WP] Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon (2024), "Beat the Market".** An intraday momentum (noise-band breakout) strategy on SPY from 2007 to early 2024 reports 1,985% total return net of costs, 19.6% annualised and Sharpe 1.33. The noise band is the open multiplied by one plus or minus the average absolute move from the open at that minute of day over the last 14 sessions; the trailing stop is the band or session VWAP; sizing targets 2% daily volatility. A secondary summary reports Sharpe 3.50 when VIX is above 40. A third-party coder initially found results weaker than the paper and came much closer after a fix. Equity-index evidence only. — [Concretum summary](https://concretumgroup.com/beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-sp500-etf-spy/); [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:chf:rpseri:rp2497); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=45565)
- **[PRAC] Replication of the Zarattini-Aziz ORB on index CFDs with costs (2026).** Timon-Pascal Krueger ran it on NQ, SPX, Dow, DAX and FTSE CFDs, cash session, January 2015 to June 2026, 2,899-2,937 sessions per market. Gross NQ expectancy was +0.131 R per trade with a 23.2% hit rate, matching the paper's roughly 0.13 R and 24%. After spread and slippage it was +0.002 R for NQ, -0.081 R for SPX, -0.081 R for Dow, -0.038 R for DAX and -0.079 R for FTSE. His conclusion: the paper is right about the pattern and wrong about the trade, because on a CFD account the edge is roughly the size of the spread. No gold in this test. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- **[PR] Holmberg, Lönnbark and Lundström (Finance Research Letters 2013).** Using crude oil futures and a bootstrap approach on the joint distribution of open, high, low and close, they find ORB returns significantly above zero and a success rate above a fair game. Abstract level only; sample period and cost treatment not confirmed. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v10y2013i1p27-33.html); [working-paper abstract](https://swopec.hhs.se/umnees/abs/umnees0845.htm)
- **[WP] Lundström, "Day trading returns across volatility states" (2013, revised 2017).** On long series of crude oil and S&P 500 futures, the average ORB return differs between the highest and lowest volatility states by around 200 basis points per day for crude oil and around 150 for the S&P 500. The author concludes day-trading success depends to a large extent on the volatility of the underlying. — [Working-paper abstract](https://swopec.hhs.se/umnees/abs/umnees0861.htm)
- **[PRAC, promotional] Gold London-range breakout.** A vendor backtest on XAUUSD, January 2023 to September 2026, real ticks in MT5: range 03:00-08:00 server time, stop orders both sides, stop at the opposite side capped at 0.6% of price, target 2.5 R, break-even at 1.5 R, time exit 21:00, one trade a day, and a filter that skips days when the range is below 0.30% or above 1.60% of price. Reported profit factor 1.37-1.38 over about 715 trades with about 49% wins and 17-19% maximum drawdown; 2026 held-out profit factor 1.61 on 157 trades. Spread, slippage and commission assumptions are not stated, and the author sells an EA built on it. Tight stops inside the range and no range filter are listed as what failed. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)

### Inferences
- The conditions that make ORB work in the literature are (a) unusually high opening volume relative to the instrument's own history, (b) high volatility states, and (c) very low costs relative to the stop distance. Gold in 2025-2026 satisfies (b) but a retail CFD account fails (c), which matches our own result (COMEX-open ORB on 5-minute bars: profit factor 1.00 gross, 0.96 net).
- The Stocks in Play result is a cross-sectional selection effect: it picks the 20 most unusual stocks out of 7,000 each day. A single instrument traded every day cannot reproduce that selection. The closest single-instrument analogue is a time-series filter: trade the breakout only on days when the opening range or opening volume is unusually large relative to the last 14 days. That is codeable from OHLCV bars, but it is untested on gold in anything I found.
- The vendor's London-breakout result (profit factor about 1.37) conflicts with our Asian-range London breakout on 15-minute bars (profit factor 0.80, and 0.82 at zero cost). The differences are a range-size filter, a wide stop at the far side of the range, a 2.5 R target with break-even, and unstated costs. Because our version loses even at zero cost, the gap is not explained by costs alone; the filter and exit structure, or overfitting on the vendor's side, must account for it. Treat the vendor figure as a hypothesis to test, not evidence.
- An ORB with a hit rate near 23-24% (as in the QQQ paper and its replication) produces long losing streaks. With a $100 account that risks 1% a trade, the account would need to survive runs of 15 or more consecutive small losses, and fixed costs per trade are a larger fraction of a small stop.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or working-paper ORB study on gold futures or spot gold was found.
- I could not retrieve a second practitioner article titled "We tested 288 versions of the gold breakout strategy on 18 years of data" ([MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777728)); the search snippet says it found no real edge, but the page content did not load, so its methods and numbers are unverified.
- A search snippet also reported a one-hour Donchian breakout on gold for 2020-2024 with profit factor 0.97, and a promotional "Initial Balance" gold strategy claiming 411% in one year; I could not verify either and have not relied on them.
- Holmberg et al. and Lundström: full texts not opened, so I cannot confirm whether their returns are net of costs.
- No Concretum study applying the ORB or noise-band method to gold futures was found.

## 3. Time-of-day and session effects in gold: London fix, overnight versus intraday returns, COMEX open and US data, intraday volatility shape

### Takeaway
Two gold-specific time-of-day facts have peer-reviewed support: gold's return has historically been earned outside US day hours (overnight positive, day session negative, 1985-2012, weakening over time), and volume and volatility peak in the London-New York overlap around US data releases. The London fix anomalies were documented under the pre-2015 fixing and should not be assumed to persist.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] Overnight versus day returns in COMEX gold.** Blose and Gondhalekar (Applied Economics Letters 2014): for the COMEX front-month contract, 1985 through 2012, overnight returns are significantly positive and day returns significantly negative; the same holds for the GLD ETF; the asymmetry has weakened substantially over the years but is still present. Abstract level; magnitudes not available. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apeclt/v21y2014i18p1269-1272.html)
- **[PR] Extension to the London fix and gold equities.** Blose, Gondhalekar and Kort (Journal of Economics and Finance 2018, 42:526-549) find the same sign pattern in COMEX futures, the London Fix spot price, gold miners, and gold closed-end funds and ETFs, in both up and down gold markets, and state it is economically important even after transaction costs. Abstract level; no magnitudes or cost assumptions available. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jecfin/v42y2018i3d10.1007_s12197-017-9403-0.html)
- **[THESIS] Around-the-clock seasonality.** Donati and Jung (Copenhagen Business School MSc thesis, 2019) report a hat-shaped intraday pattern: gold appreciates during eastern (Asian) trading hours and depreciates for the rest of the day, with price drops around the London fixing times in the years of alleged manipulation. Their strategies reach Sharpe ratios up to 1.61 without costs; with costs they underperform the market while remaining somewhat profitable. Not peer-reviewed; abstract only. — [CBS thesis record](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/gold-price-dynamics-around-the-clock/)
- **[PR] London PM fix, old regime.** Caminschi and Heaney (Journal of Futures Markets 2014, 34:1003-1039) study GC futures and the GLD ETF around the London PM fixing. Volume and volatility rise significantly immediately after the fixing starts, well before the result is published; returns to informed traders are significant in the first 4 minutes; trades in the opening minutes predict the fix direction, in some cases with more than 90% accuracy; nothing significant happens after publication. This is a pre-2015 result under the five-bank telephone fixing. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v34y2014i11p1003-1039.html)
- **[WP, reported via LBMA] Abrantes-Metz and Metz.** An unpublished draft using intraday data 2001-2013 found unusual activity around 15:00 London from 2004 onward and none at the morning fix. On days with large moves during the fix, the moves were downward at least two-thirds of the time in six of the years 2004-2013, and 92% of the time in 2010. Pre-2015 regime. — [LBMA Alchemist, issue 73](https://www.lbma.org.uk/alchemist/issue-73/has-there-been-a-decade-of-london-pm-gold-fixing-manipulation)
- **[PRAC, industry rebuttal] Alternative explanation.** Peter Fertig, writing in the LBMA's journal, argues the PM pattern is explained by the London-New York overlap, US data released at 10:00 New York time (15:00 London), and central-bank and miner selling concentrated at the fix, and is not proof of manipulation. — [LBMA Alchemist, issue 73](https://www.lbma.org.uk/alchemist/issue-73/has-there-been-a-decade-of-london-pm-gold-fixing-manipulation)
- **[Primary] The fix changed in March 2015.** The London Gold Fixing was replaced by the LBMA Gold Price, an electronic auction run by ICE Benchmark Administration at 10:30 and 15:00 London time. — [ICE methodology document](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/LBMA_Gold_Price_and_LBMA_Silver_Price_Calculation_Methodology.pdf)
- **[PR] Post-reform study exists but I could not read it.** Aspris, Foley and O'Neill, "Benchmarks in the spotlight" (Journal of Futures Markets, November 2020), examine how the move to electronic precious-metal fixes affected market quality and price discovery. — [Record](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/benchmarks-in-the-spotlight-855596657)
- **[PR] Intraday shape of volume, volatility and spread in spot gold.** Batten, Lucey, McGroarty, Peat and Urquhart (PLoS ONE 2017), 5-minute Thomson Reuters data, 1 May 2000 to 30 April 2015: trade counts are highest roughly 11:00-17:00 GMT (the Europe-North America overlap); volatility is roughly constant until about 12:00 GMT, rises until about 14:00 GMT, then falls and levels off; the bid-ask spread is largely flat through the day with a small rise around the 21:00-22:00 GMT daily break. The paper's mean bid-ask spread measure fell from 0.00185 in 2000-2005 to 0.000600 in 2010-2015. The mean 5-minute return was negative in 2010-2015. — [PLoS ONE full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0174232)
- **[PR] Where liquidity sat in the futures data.** Baltussen et al. set the gold futures "trading day" at 08:20-13:30 ET because that is where volume concentrated in their 1984-2020 sample. — [Baltussen et al., Table A3](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **[PR] US data releases.** Cai, Cheung and Wong, "What moves the gold market?" (Journal of Futures Markets 2001): intraday patterns strongly shape volatility, and of 23 US announcements, employment reports, GDP, CPI and personal income had the largest effects. Published 2001, so the data predate electronic trading. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v21y2001i3p257-278.html)
- **[PR, attribution partly unconfirmed] 08:30 ET releases in 5-minute data.** A study of gold, silver and copper futures reports that the 08:30 ET announcements, particularly nonfarm payrolls and durable goods, produce a swift and significant response. I identified this from a search snippet and did not open the full text. — [Repository copy](https://mountainscholar.org/bitstreams/21623442-a01d-45a6-879d-d645e781e52a/download)
- **[PR] Asymmetry of the news response.** Smales and Yang (International Review of Financial Analysis 2015): gold futures react more to good economic news (which is negative for gold), and the reaction is larger when forecaster disagreement is wider. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finana:v:41:y:2015:i:c:p:292-302)
- **[PR] Price discovery by venue.** A University of Technology Sydney paper on 1997-2014 intraday data finds both the London over-the-counter market and US futures contribute to gold price discovery, with market structure mattering. — [UTS repository copy](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/41414/4/GoldILS%20JFutMkt%20Forthcoming.pdf)
- **[PR, FX as supporting evidence] Time-of-day drift exists in currencies.** Breedon and Ranaldo (Journal of Money, Credit and Banking 2013) document that currencies tend to depreciate during their own local trading hours, mirrored in order flow, and an earlier draft says the pattern supports a simple profitable EUR/USD strategy. Whether it survives costs was not confirmed. — [SNB working paper](https://www.snb.ch/en/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04); [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jmoncb:v:45:y:2013:i:5:p:953-965)

### Inferences
- The "gold is earned in Asian hours" claim has real support for 1985-2012, but three things weaken it as a scalping basis: the authors themselves say it has weakened substantially, the sample ends before the 2015 fix reform, and our own 2019-2026 hourly test of an Asia-session long drift went from profit factor 1.12 gross to 0.98 net. The literature and our test agree: a small drift exists and costs consume it.
- An overnight-drift trade is one position held for hours. It is a session-bias strategy, not a scalp, and its edge per trade is a few basis points at best. It does suggest a directional filter: long-only signals taken in Asian hours and caution with longs in the US morning. That is an untested hypothesis.
- For a 5- or 15-minute method, the most reliable time-of-day fact is about volatility, not direction: range and volume concentrate from about 12:00 to 17:00 GMT, peaking around 13:30-14:00 GMT. In Singapore that is roughly 20:00 to 01:00. Since spreads are roughly flat through the day while range expands, the ratio of cost to expected move is lowest in that window. Any rule should be tested with a session filter restricted to those hours.
- The London fix patterns (downward bias at 15:00 London, front-running in the first 4 minutes) come from a mechanism that was abolished in March 2015. No study I found shows they persist under the electronic auction, so "fade or follow the fix" should be classed as folklore until tested on post-2015 data.
- News-release evidence shows fast, significant reactions at 08:30 ET, but nothing I found shows a tradable continuation or reversal after the first minutes from bar data. Excluding the bars around 08:30 ET releases is a defensible risk rule; trading them is not supported.

### Gaps
- Magnitudes for the overnight and day returns (per day or per year) in Blose et al. are behind a paywall and were not retrieved.
- I found no formal study that splits gold returns into Asia, London and New York segments after 2015 with statistical tests. A July 2026 commentary titled "A Strange Dichotomy: Gold Up on the Year in Asian Markets, Down Big in the West" ([Money Metals](https://www.moneymetals.com/news/2026/07/12/a-strange-dichotomy-gold-up-on-the-year-in-asian-markets-down-big-in-the-west-005059)) indicates the pattern is still being discussed in 2026, but the page returned HTTP 403 and its numbers are unverified.
- No Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka style intraday return-periodicity study (same half-hour predicting itself on later days) was found for gold. Batten et al. (2017) state that returns show periodicity but give no hour-by-hour averages.
- No study of the COMEX 08:20 ET open as a distinct event in the electronic era was found.
- Findings of Aspris, Foley and O'Neill (2020) on the post-2015 auction were not accessible.
- The exact intraday volatility shape for 2025-2026 is not in the literature; it should be measured directly from our own bars.

## 4. Mean reversion at short horizons: is there 5-60 minute reversal in gold or FX, and is it tradable after spread?

### Takeaway
I found no gold-specific study that documents a tradable 5-60 minute reversal after costs. The one cross-asset test of large intraday moves in commodity futures finds same-day continuation, not reversal, with the reversal arriving over the following days.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] Large intraday moves in commodity futures continue the same day.** Baltussen et al. define a jump as a half-hour in which the return from the prior close falls below the 10th or above the 90th percentile of that asset's daily returns. For commodity futures, the cumulative return up to the jump positively predicts the return from the jump to the close: slope 3.28 (x100), t = 3.55, R² 0.70%; and it still predicts the last half-hour: slope 1.00 (x100), t = 2.54. Pooled across 21 commodities, 1974-2020, gross. — [Baltussen et al., Table 10](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **[PR] The reversal comes later.** The same paper reports that the last-half-hour momentum move "reverts over the next days", consistent with temporary price pressure. — [EconPapers abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403)
- **[WP, daily data] Next-day contrarian effect in gold.** A CESifo working paper on gold and oil, 2009-2020, reports a contrarian effect in gold on the day after abnormal returns and says trading simulations can exploit it. This is a daily-horizon result, and I did not confirm whether the simulations include costs. — [CESifo Working Paper 8445](https://cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp8445.pdf)
- **[WP, equities as supporting evidence] Intraday reversal after extreme moves in liquid stocks.** A study of liquid NYSE and NASDAQ stocks finds significant reversal after both intraday price drops and rises, and notes that in earlier daily-data work the bid-ask spread absorbs the profit. Not gold. — [arXiv cond-mat/0406696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0406696)
- **[PR] No autocorrelation evidence in the main gold stylised-facts paper.** Batten et al. (2017), using 5-minute spot gold data for 2000-2015, do not report return autocorrelations; they report that returns are negatively correlated with contemporaneous and near-future volatility. — [PLoS ONE full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0174232)
- **[PR] Intraday moving-average evidence points away from fast reversal rules.** In 5-minute spot gold, the four commonly used fast settings (1-50, 1-100, 1-150, 1-200 bars) lost money for simple, exponential and weighted averages, 2008-2014, gross. That shows short-lookback trend rules fail; it does not show that the opposite (fading) rule profits after costs. — [CXO Advisory summary of Urquhart et al. 2015](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=27613)

### Inferences
- Our own test is the most relevant data point: RSI(2) pullback with a 200-SMA filter on hourly bars had profit factor 1.09 gross and 0.82 net for 2019-2026. That is the standard pattern for short-horizon reversal: a small gross edge, much of it likely bid-ask bounce, that disappears once the spread is paid twice.
- A pure "fade the spike" rule on gold has no academic support at the intraday horizon, and the best evidence (jump continuation in commodities) points the other way within the day. The evidence favours "large morning move, same-direction drift into the close" over "large move, snap back".
- Reversal strategies typically have small average wins relative to the spread. On a 5-minute gold chart, a $2-3 mean-reversion target with $0.30-0.50 total round-trip cost gives away 10-25% of gross profit per winning trade, which is why such strategies need a very high win rate to survive. This is arithmetic on assumed costs, not a cited result.
- If a mean-reversion element is wanted, the defensible form is a pullback entry in the direction of the session trend (buying a dip toward session VWAP or a moving average on a day already moving up), because it combines the supported effect (intraday continuation) with a cheaper entry. No gold study tests this exact rule.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of 5-60 minute return autocorrelation or reversal in spot gold or GC futures with realistic costs was found. This is a genuine gap in the literature I could reach, not just a search failure: four differently phrased searches returned nothing gold-specific.
- No study of post-news-spike overreaction in gold (for example fading the first 5 minutes after payrolls) with costs was found.
- FX evidence on 5-60 minute reversal after costs was not retrieved in this session.
- A search summary mentioned a EuroStoxx 50 futures study finding intraday anti-persistence (reversal) with monthly persistence, but I could not attribute it to a specific paper, so it is not cited.

## 5. Trend versus mean-reversion regime: is intraday gold more trending or mean-reverting, and does it vary by session or volatility?

### Takeaway
There is no clean answer in the literature: I found no variance-ratio or Hurst study of intraday gold by session. What evidence exists says intraday continuation effects are concentrated in high-volatility, high-volume conditions, and that gold's intraday efficiency changes with regime.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] 15-minute gold, multifractal analysis.** Mensi, Sensoy, Vo and Kang (Resources Policy 2020) analyse 15-minute gold CFD prices from 23 April 2018 to 24 April 2020. Their test of whether the generalised Hurst exponent H(2) equals 0.5 gives t = -2.18 (p = 0.041) for the whole period, t = 1.80 (p = 0.086) before COVID-19, and t = 4.09 (p < 0.001) during COVID-19. The market-deficiency measure for gold rose from 0.1047 before to 0.1688 during the outbreak. The paper concludes that gold and oil were inefficient, particularly during the outbreak, and that efficiency depends on scale and trend direction. It does not state the H(2) values themselves or classify gold as persistent or anti-persistent. — [Full text on PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7420105)
- **[PR] Continuation is stronger in volatile, high-volume openings (Chinese metals).** Jin et al. (2020) report the strongest first-to-last half-hour momentum on days with high opening volume or volatility. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v40y2020i4p632-650.html)
- **[PR/WP] Same finding for Chinese gold and silver.** Higher volatility and lower liquidity go with stronger intraday momentum. Search-summary level. — [Working-paper version](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/686816/4a-Z-Ivy-Zhou.pdf)
- **[WP] Breakout returns scale with volatility state.** Lundström finds ORB returns about 200 basis points per day higher in the highest than in the lowest volatility state for crude oil futures, and about 150 for S&P 500 futures. — [Working-paper abstract](https://swopec.hhs.se/umnees/abs/umnees0861.htm)
- **[WP, secondary summary] Equity intraday momentum is strongest in crisis volatility.** The SPY noise-band strategy's Sharpe ratio is reported as 3.50 when VIX exceeds 40, against 1.33 overall. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=45565)
- **[PR] Predictability in precious metals is time-varying.** A study using variance-ratio tests, the BDS test and the Hurst exponent finds precious-metal return predictability varies over time, consistent with the Adaptive Market Hypothesis. This is not an intraday-by-session analysis. — [Accepted manuscript, European Journal of Finance](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/396646/1/EJF_accepted_Manuscript.docx)
- **[PR, daily data] Variance-ratio tests on 28 gold markets.** Ntim et al. (International Review of Financial Analysis 2015) find weak-form efficiency is rejected more often in emerging than in developed gold markets. Daily spot data, not intraday. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finana:v:41:y:2015:i:c:p:218-236)
- **[PR] Gold intraday trend rules that worked were slow.** In 5-minute gold, 2008-2014, the best moving-average pairs were 44-332 (simple), 49-259 (exponential) and 49-498 (weighted) bars. — [CXO Advisory summary of Urquhart et al. 2015](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=27613)

### Inferences
- Reading the Mensi et al. test statistics by sign suggests 15-minute gold was close to a random walk or slightly anti-persistent in calm 2018-2019 and persistent (trending) during the 2020 volatility shock. The paper does not state the direction of its test statistic, so this reading is tentative.
- Across four separate studies (Chinese metals, crude oil ORB, SPY noise bands, gold multifractality) the common thread is that intraday trend-following pays in high-volatility states and not in calm ones. This is the best-supported conditional statement available, and it is codeable: a volatility-state filter such as today's opening range or yesterday's ATR relative to a 14-20 day average.
- That inference is in tension with our own 2025-2026 result, where naive breakouts failed in a high-volatility regime. Two explanations fit: the volatility premium for breakouts is real but smaller than the cost per trade on a retail gold account, or 2025-2026 gold volatility came with sharp two-way reversals (gold is about 27% below its 2026 high) rather than one-way trend days. Our backtester can separate these by testing breakouts only on days in the top volatility tercile and by looking at gross and net results separately.
- The best 5-minute gold moving-average pairs correspond to a fast average of about 3.7-4.1 hours and a slow average of about 22-42 hours. The intraday trend that had statistical support is a multi-hour to multi-day trend observed on 5-minute bars, not a 5-minute scalping signal. A higher-timeframe trend filter (for example the 1-hour or 4-hour direction) is better supported than fast crossovers on the trading timeframe.
- One paragraph of daily and weekly context, as requested: large rule-universe studies on daily data find technical trend rules were profitable historically and have decayed. Marshall, Cahan and Cahan found no reliable profit in 14 of 15 commodity futures after data-snooping adjustment (1984-2005), and Hsu, Taylor and Wang found only 3 of 9 developed-market currencies predictable at 90% confidence with diminishing profitability over time (sources in section 6). Slow trend-following in gold is a separate topic and is not evidence for intraday scalping.

### Gaps
- No variance-ratio or Hurst-exponent study of gold at 5-, 15- or 60-minute horizons split by session (Asia, London, New York) was found.
- No study of intraday trend versus reversal in gold for the 2024-2026 regime was found.
- The numerical H(2) values in Mensi et al. are only in a figure, so the size of any departure from 0.5 is unknown.

## 6. Technical trading rules on intraday gold and FX: what do large-scale tests with data-snooping controls find?

### Takeaway
One gold-specific intraday study exists: of 66,297 moving-average rules on 5-minute spot gold (2008-2014), the popular fast settings lost money, only slow simple-moving-average rules were significant in and out of sample, and all results were before costs. In FX and commodity futures, intraday and daily rule profits largely vanish after realistic costs and data-snooping adjustment.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] Gold-specific intraday rule test.** Batten, Lucey, McGroarty, Peat and Urquhart, "Does intraday technical trading have predictive power in precious metal markets?" (Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions and Money 2018, 52:102-113): intraday moving-average rules have some predictive power in gold and no significant predictability in silver. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfin/v52y2018icp102-113.html)
- **[WP, via secondary summary] Detail from the 2015 working-paper version.** Data: 5-minute spot gold and silver, January 2008 to mid-September 2014. Rules: 66,297 simple, exponential and weighted moving-average crossovers with fast average 1-49 bars and slow average 50-500 bars. The four standard settings (1-50, 1-100, 1-150, 1-200) produced negative returns for all three average types on both metals. For gold, 56.4% of simple-average rules had positive gross returns but only 20.2% were significantly positive; for exponential and weighted averages only 1.3% and 1.9% were significantly positive. Best gold rules: 44-332 (simple), 49-259 (exponential), 49-498 (weighted). — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=27613)
- **[WP, via secondary summary] Out-of-sample and costs.** With an in-sample period ending August 2010, the best in-sample gold rules were profitable out of sample, but only the simple-average rules were statistically significant in both sub-periods; the best silver rules were unprofitable out of sample; a bootstrap generally confirmed the findings. All returns are gross, the authors describe intraday moving-average trading in gold as possible but not easy, and the summary notes trading costs could change the findings. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=27613)
- **[PR, FX as supporting evidence] Intraday FX rules do not survive costs.** Neely and Weller (Journal of International Money and Finance 2003) select intraday FX rules with a genetic program and an optimised linear model. Once realistic transaction costs and trading hours are imposed they find no evidence of excess returns, even though the rules detect remarkably stable patterns. The data are from the 1990s. — [St. Louis Fed working paper record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fip:fedlwp:1999-016); [journal record](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/neely_2003_JIMF.html)
- **[PR, FX, daily data] Largest data-snooping-controlled FX study.** Hsu, Taylor and Wang (Journal of International Economics 2016, 102:188-208) test over 21,000 rules on daily data for 30 currencies over 45 years with a stepwise test. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:inecon:v:102:y:2016:i:c:p:188-208)
- **[PRAC, secondary summary of Hsu et al.]** Three of nine developed-market currencies are predictable at 90% confidence; the yen is most profitable (annualised Sharpe 0.75); effectiveness diminishes over time and is stronger in emerging currencies; one-way costs averaged 0.045% for developed markets. This is daily, not intraday, evidence. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=28457)
- **[PR, commodity futures, daily data] Marshall, Cahan and Cahan (Journal of Banking and Finance 2008, 32:1810-1819).** — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v32y2008i9p1810-1819.html)
- **[PRAC, secondary summary of Marshall et al.]** 7,846 rules from five families (filter, moving average, support and resistance, channel breakout, on-balance volume) on 15 commodity futures, 1984-2005. The best rule per commodity was significant on its own, but adjusting for data snooping removes reliable profits for 14 of 15 commodities. The authors conclude the rules are not profitable as stand-alone methods. Daily data, pre-2006. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/does-technical-trading-work-with-commodity-futures/)
- **[PRAC] A current illustration of the cost point.** The 2026 CFD replication of the equity ORB found the gross edge intact and the net edge zero. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)

### Inferences
- A gold-specific cost check on the Batten et al. result is missing from the paper, so here is the arithmetic. A 1-50 bar rule on 5-minute data crosses many times a day; at roughly $0.30-0.50 per round trip, a rule that is already negative gross cannot be rescued. The slow rules (for example 44-332 bars) trade far less often, perhaps a few times a week, so cost per trade is a much smaller share of the expected move. Cost per trade relative to holding period is the deciding variable, which is what our own backtests showed.
- The published gold result is from 2008-2014, a period containing one large bull run to 2011 and a crash in 2013. A slow moving-average rule that is long in a bull run and short in a crash will look good; this is closer to daily trend-following measured on 5-minute bars than to an intraday edge.
- No study tests channel (Donchian), Bollinger, Keltner, RSI or VWAP rules on intraday gold with data-snooping control. For those rule families the correct statement is "no gold-specific evidence", not "evidence against".
- Because the academic tests cover tens of thousands of rules and find only a small subset significant, any single rule we pick and tune on 2025-2026 data is subject to the same data-snooping problem. A fair test fixes parameters in advance from the literature (for example a slow trend filter and a volatility filter), uses a held-out period, and reports the number of variants tried.

### Gaps
- The full text of Batten et al. (2018) could not be opened: the Reading repository mirror did not resolve from this session and the main repository page was bot-blocked. Return magnitudes and any breakeven-cost analysis in the published version are therefore unverified.
- Which single commodity survived data-snooping adjustment in Marshall et al. was not confirmed; I did not verify whether it was gold.
- I did not retrieve Marshall, Cahan and Cahan's separate study of intraday technical analysis in US equities, so it is not cited.
- No intraday gold study using White's Reality Check or Hansen's SPA test by name was found; Batten et al. use bootstrap and out-of-sample checks instead.

## 7. Retail reality: what do the best studies say about day-trader and leveraged CFD profitability, and is there evidence specific to scalping, high leverage or very small accounts?

### Takeaway
Across Brazil, Taiwan, India, France, the UK, Ireland and the EU, 70-97% of retail day traders and CFD clients lose money, fewer than 1-3% earn a meaningful income, losses are worse for the most frequent and most leveraged traders, and there is no evidence of learning with persistence. No study isolates $100 accounts or gold scalping.

### Cited Findings
- **[WP] Brazil, complete regulator data.** Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?": of individuals who began day trading Brazilian equity index futures in 2013-2015 and persisted for more than 300 days, 97% lost money, 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage and 0.5% earned more than a bank teller's starting salary. The authors find no evidence of learning. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)
- **[PRAC summary of the same paper]** The sample is 19,646 first-time day traders from securities-regulator records; the top earner averaged US$310 a day; among people who traded for only one day, 29.8% had a positive net profit, and profitability fell the longer people kept trading. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)
- **[PR] Taiwan, all day traders 1992-2006.** Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean (Journal of Financial Markets 2014): fewer than 1% of day traders predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees; the top 500 ranked on prior-year performance earn about 37.9 basis points a day after fees. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24); [working-paper PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- **[WP] Taiwan, persistence without profit.** The aggregate performance of day traders net of fees is negative in each of the 15 years studied, and unprofitable experienced traders continue at nearly the same rate as profitable ones. — [Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, learning paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- **[REG] EU.** ESMA's 2018 product intervention cited national-regulator studies showing 74-89% of retail CFD accounts typically lose money, with average losses per client of €1,600 to €29,000. It capped retail leverage between 30:1 and 2:1 by underlying, with 20:1 for gold. — [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)
- **[REG, via trade press] France.** The AMF's 2014 study of FX and CFD clients found 89% lost money over 2009-2012, with an average loss of about €10,900 per client. — [Finance Magnates report](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/analysis/amf-publishes-four-year-fx-study-89-of-investors-experiencing-losses/); [AMF savings-observatory letter, October 2014](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/lettre_ou_cahier/lettre_observatoire/La%20lettre%20de%20l%27Observatoire%20de%20l%27epargne%20de%20l%27AMF%20-%20ndeg%2010%20-%20Octobre%202014.pdf)
- **[REG] UK.** The FCA's analysis of a representative sample of CFD-firm client accounts found 82% of clients lost money. — [FCA press release, December 2016](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-proposes-stricter-rules-contract-difference-products)
- **[REG, via press] Ireland.** The Central Bank of Ireland found 75% of retail CFD clients lost money in 2013-2014 and 74% in a 2016 follow-up. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/personal-finance/europe-restricts-leverage-on-cfds-to-protect-retail-investors-1.3441764)
- **[REG] India, derivatives.** SEBI's September 2024 study: 93% of more than 1 crore (10 million) individual equity futures-and-options traders lost money over FY22-FY24, with aggregate losses above ₹1.8 lakh crore including transaction costs. — [SEBI press release](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html)
- **[REG, via press] India, intraday equity; frequency and size effects.** SEBI's July 2024 study: 71% of individual intraday traders in the equity cash segment lost money in FY23; the share rises to 80% for very frequent traders and 76% for traders under 30; the press report's headline states the impact is higher among young and small traders. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/71-of-intraday-traders-incur-losses-finds-sebi-report-impact-higher-among-young-small-traders/2192598)
- **[PR] Leverage causally worsens retail outcomes.** Heimer and Simsek (Journal of Financial Economics 2019) study the 2010 US cap on retail FX leverage: it cut trading volume by 23% and reduced high-leverage traders' losses by 40%, without changing the bid-ask prices brokers charged. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:132:y:2019:i:3:p:1-21); [NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24176)
- **[Press] Singapore.** MAS raised the minimum margin for retail leveraged FX from 2% to 5%, a 20:1 cap, effective 8 October 2019; the 2% minimum still applies to accredited, expert and institutional investors. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/regulation/fx-leverage-cut-to-201-in-singapore/); [Yahoo Finance Singapore](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/fx-margin-rules-kick-oct-085800068.html)
- **[PRAC] The cost mechanism in one line.** In the 2026 CFD replication of a published day-trading strategy, the gross edge of about 0.13 R per trade became about zero after spread and slippage. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)

### Inferences
- The base rate for the trader's stated plan (frequent, leveraged, intraday, small account) is the worst cell in every dataset: SEBI's loss share rises with trading frequency, Heimer and Simsek show leverage raises losses, and Chague et al. show persistence does not help. A realistic prior for "grow $100 by scalping gold" is a loss probability above 80-90% over a year.
- Account-size arithmetic at $4,050 gold: the usual minimum CFD size of 0.01 lot is 1 ounce, so $1 of price movement is $1, or 1% of a $100 account, and the $75 daily ATR is 75% of the account. Under a 20:1 leverage cap, margin for 0.01 lot is about $202, more than the whole account. A $100 account can therefore only trade spot gold through a broker offering much higher leverage than ESMA-style caps allow, or through smaller-than-0.01 contract sizes. Higher leverage is exactly the condition the Heimer-Simsek result links to larger losses.
- Risking 1% ($1) per trade at 0.01 lot means a $1 stop, which on a 5-minute gold chart with a $75 daily ATR is inside ordinary noise. A stop wide enough to be meaningful ($5-10) risks 5-10% of the account per trade. The account size, not the choice of indicator, is the binding constraint, and the report should say so plainly.
- Cross-cutting ranking of strategy types by strength of evidence for intraday gold. (1) Session-volatility timing, meaning trading only 12:00-17:00 GMT: strong peer-reviewed gold evidence that range and volume concentrate there, and it is a filter rather than an edge. (2) Intraday continuation conditioned on a strong session move and a high-volatility state: statistically supported for gold futures (Baltussen et al.) and across several markets, small, gross, never shown net for gold. (3) Slow trend filter from a higher timeframe: the only moving-average result that held out of sample on 5-minute gold. (4) Overnight or Asian-session long drift: peer-reviewed for 1985-2012, weakening, and negative net in our own test. (5) Opening-range breakout on gold: no academic gold evidence, negative in our tests. (6) Short-horizon reversal or fading spikes: no gold evidence, and the commodity evidence points to same-day continuation. (7) London-fix timing: documented only under a mechanism abolished in 2015. (8) Fast moving-average crossovers on the trading timeframe: tested on gold and negative even before costs.
- The single rule-based candidate most consistent with the evidence is therefore a trend-day continuation method on 15-minute bars: trade only in the London-New York overlap, only in the direction of a higher-timeframe trend, only when the session has already moved by more than a volatility-scaled threshold from its open (the noise-band idea), enter on a pullback toward session VWAP rather than at the breakout price, use an ATR-scaled stop wide enough that round-trip cost is a small fraction of risk, and exit at a fixed session time. Every component has some support; the combination is untested on gold, and the literature says to expect a gross edge of a few basis points per trade at best. On 15-minute rather than 5-minute bars the same cost is spread over a larger expected move.
- The honest framing for the report: the academic record supports testing this as a hypothesis with pre-fixed parameters and a held-out period, and it does not support an expectation of profit. If the net profit factor comes out near 1.0, that is the expected result, not a failure of the test.

### Gaps
- No study isolates accounts of about $100, scalping as a style, or retail gold CFD trading specifically. The regulator figures pool all CFD products and all account sizes.
- I did not find a MAS publication stating the share of Singapore retail CFD accounts that lose money, nor a primary MAS source for a gold-specific retail margin requirement; the 20:1 figure above is the FX rule as reported by trade press. The 20:1 gold figure cited is ESMA's.
- The AMF figures come from trade-press reports of the study; another report gives the window as 2009-2013 with an average loss of €10,887, and a later AMF release says 90%, so small discrepancies exist between sources.
- Broker-published quarterly profitability percentages (for example the loss-rate disclosures on EU broker websites) were not collected.
- Source-access limits in this session: reading-clone.eprints-hosting.org did not resolve (it would need to be allowed in the session's network settings), the Reading and Hull repositories, the AUT working paper and Money Metals returned bot-blocks or HTTP 403, and the Springer and Wiley full texts are paywalled. Findings from those papers are therefore at abstract or secondary-summary level, as flagged above.
